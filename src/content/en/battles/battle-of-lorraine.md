The [Battle of Lorraine](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_o_Lotaryngię) was the French army's first great offensive of the First World War and the first of the so-called [Battles of the Frontiers](/bitwy/bitwa-graniczna). It began on 14 August 1914, and its decisive phase was fought on 20 August at Morhange and Sarrebourg.

## The plan of the offensive

The French war plan envisaged a rapid advance eastwards into [Lorraine](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotaryngia), lost in 1871. The region was rich in coal and iron ore, and its recovery mattered enormously to French national pride. The high command believed that a determined attack, pressed home with full commitment, would break the German defences.

Two armies went into the attack: the 1st Army of General [Auguste Dubail](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Dubail), heading for [Sarrebourg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarrebourg), and the 2nd Army of General [Noël de Castelnau](/postacie/noel-de-castelnau), advancing towards [Morhange](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morhange). Facing them stood the German 6th Army under the heir to the Bavarian throne, Crown Prince Rupprecht, and the 7th Army of General [Josias von Heeringen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josias_von_Heeringen).

## The trap at Morhange

For the first few days the French advanced almost unopposed. The Germans were deliberately falling back, drawing the enemy deep into well-prepared ground. On 20 August they went over to a massive counter-attack. The French infantry, advancing in close order and in red trousers, came under the fire of heavy artillery and machine guns from fortified positions.

The 2nd Army suffered a heavy defeat at Morhange. Among its formations was the XX Corps of General [Ferdinand Foch](/postacie/ferdinand-foch), later Supreme Commander of the Allied forces. General Castelnau's son was also killed in this fighting. The 1st Army, too, had to withdraw from Sarrebourg.

## The defence of Nancy

The French withdrew across the border to the wooded hills in front of [Nancy](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy). The Germans tried to follow up their success, but in fighting that lasted until 25 August the French armies halted the pursuit. At the beginning of September the Germans attacked Nancy again, but this attack also failed.

## Significance

The Battle of Lorraine showed how high a price the French army paid for its faith in the offensive at all costs. French losses were enormous, and pre-war doctrine had to be revised quickly. At the same time, holding the line in front of Nancy secured the right wing of the French army, which proved important a few weeks later during the [Battle of the Marne](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_bitwa_nad_Marną).
