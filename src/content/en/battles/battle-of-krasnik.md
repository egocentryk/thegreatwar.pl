The [Battle of Kraśnik](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Kraśnikiem) was fought on 23–25 August 1914 in the southern Lublin region, in lands then belonging to the Kingdom of Poland under Russian rule. It was the first clash of the great [Battle of Galicia](/bitwy/bitwa-galicyjska) and the first victory of the Austro-Hungarian army in the First World War.

## Offensive to the north

The Chief of the Austro-Hungarian General Staff, Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf, decided to strike north from Galicia, deep into the Kingdom of Poland, between the Vistula and the Bug. He hoped to smash the Russian armies before Russia could fully mobilise its forces. On the left wing of the offensive the Austro-Hungarian 1st Army of General Viktor Dankl was advancing.

The Russians were also preparing an attack. Their 4th Army, commanded by General Anton Saltza, was marching south towards Galicia. Neither side knew exactly where the enemy's main forces were.

## Course of the battle

On 23 August the two armies collided south of [Kraśnik](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraśnik), in hilly terrain cut by ravines. The Austro-Hungarians had numerical superiority and from the start pushed back the Russian right wing. On 24 August the fighting spread along the whole front. On 25 August Dankl concentrated his attack on his left wing, and the Russians, threatened with envelopment, began to withdraw towards [Lublin](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin).

According to historians' estimates, Austria-Hungary lost about 15,000 soldiers and the Russians about 25,000, including 6,000 prisoners.

## Significance

Kraśnik gave Austria-Hungary a moment of joy after the defeat in Serbia. Dankl became a hero and was later given a title with the suffix "von Krasnik". General Saltza lost his command and was replaced by General [Alexei Evert](/postacie/aleksiej-ewert). A few days later the Austro-Hungarian 4th Army won a victory at Komarów.

The successes in the north proved short-lived, however. To the east, near Lemberg, the Russians were routing the weaker Austro-Hungarian armies, and at the beginning of September they took the city. After unsuccessful fighting at Rava-Ruska, Conrad had to order the whole front to retreat behind the San. Many Poles fought on both sides of the front, as conscripts in the Russian and Austro-Hungarian armies.
