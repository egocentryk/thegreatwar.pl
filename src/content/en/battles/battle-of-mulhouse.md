The [Battle of Mulhouse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Miluzą_(1914)) was the first French offensive of the First World War. It was fought in two stages, on 7–10 and 14–25 August 1914, on the plain of southern [Alsace](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzacja), between the Vosges and the Rhine.

## The fight for a lost province

Alsace had belonged to Germany since 1871, when France lost it after its defeat in the Franco-Prussian War. Regaining Alsace and Lorraine was a matter of national pride for the French. The French war plan therefore provided from the outset for an offensive to the east, and the attack on [Mulhouse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miluza) was to open the way to the Rhine and arouse enthusiasm at home.

## The first entry

On 7 August the French VII Corps of General Louis Bonneau set out from the Belfort area and took [Altkirch](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altkirch). On 8 August the French entered Mulhouse, where some of the inhabitants welcomed them joyfully. The Commander-in-Chief, [Joseph Joffre](/postacie/joseph-joffre), announced in a proclamation that French soldiers were arriving as the first workers of the great work of revenge.

The joy was short-lived. The German Seventh Army of General [Josias von Heeringen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josias_von_Heeringen) counter-attacked, and the French were threatened with encirclement. On 10 August they had to leave the city and withdraw towards Belfort. Joffre blamed Bonneau for the defeat and removed him from command. He was one of the first of the many generals Joffre dismissed in the opening weeks of the war.

## The second attempt

On 14 August the Army of Alsace, formed specially for the purpose under General [Paul Pau](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pau), went into the attack. This time the French acted more cautiously and in greater strength. On 19 August they occupied Mulhouse again.

The fate of Alsace, however, was decided elsewhere. In Lorraine, the Ardennes and Belgium the French armies were suffering defeats, and the German thrust through Belgium threatened France itself. Joffre needed every soldier in the north. From 22 August units of the Army of Alsace were transferred to other sectors of the front. Pau's weakened troops left Mulhouse without a fight and withdrew to a line near Belfort, and on 25 August the Germans occupied the city again. Sources give different dates for the evacuation, from 24 to 26 August. On 28 August the Army of Alsace was disbanded.

## Significance

The offensive in Alsace had little military importance but considerable symbolic meaning. It showed the French desire to regain the lost lands, and at the same time the weaknesses of French command in the first days of the war. Until the end of the war the French held only a small strip of southern Alsace. Mulhouse returned to France only in November 1918.
