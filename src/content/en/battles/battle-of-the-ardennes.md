The Battle of the [Ardennes](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardeny), fought on 21–24 August 1914, was one of the bloodiest episodes of the [Battle of the Frontiers](/bitwy/bitwa-graniczna). It included 22 August, the day on which the French army suffered the heaviest losses in its entire history.

## A blind blow

The Commander-in-Chief, [Joseph Joffre](/postacie/joseph-joffre), believed that the Germans had sent their main forces through northern Belgium and that their centre in the Ardennes was weak. He decided to strike precisely there, through the wooded, hilly country of the Belgian province of Luxembourg, in order to cut the German front in two. The Third Army of General [Pierre Ruffey](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Ruffey) and the Fourth Army of General [Fernand de Langle de Cary](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_de_Langle_de_Cary) went into the attack.

The assumption was wrong. In the Ardennes stood two strong German armies: the Fourth Army of Duke [Albrecht of Württemberg](/postacie/albrecht-wirtemberski) and the Fifth Army of Crown Prince [Wilhelm](/postacie/wilhelm-nastepca-tronu). The French had poor reconnaissance, lacked accurate maps, and the dense forests and morning mists limited visibility.

## The black day of 22 August

On 22 August French columns marching through the forests unexpectedly ran into German units ready for battle. In many places at once, among them near Rossignol, [Virton](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virton) and Neufchâteau and in the area of [Longwy](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longwy), violent, chaotic clashes broke out. The French infantry, in bright uniforms and attacking with the bayonet, fell under machine-gun and artillery fire.

The Colonial Corps suffered especially heavy losses at Rossignol, where one of its divisions was almost destroyed. That day about 27,000 French soldiers were killed along the whole front of the Battle of the Frontiers. That is more than on any other single day of fighting in the history of France.

## Retreat

On 23 August the French armies began to withdraw, fighting delaying actions. On 24 August Joffre ordered the Fourth Army to retreat behind the [Meuse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moza), and the Third Army, after a failed attempt to counter-attack with its right wing, also fell back towards the river and Verdun. English-language literature usually dates the battle to 21–23 August, but French regimental histories also include the fighting of 24 August, and Gabriel Hanotaux in 1917 extended it to 25 August.

The defeat in the Ardennes coincided with the defeats at Charleroi and Mons, which forced the entire left wing of the Allied armies into the great retreat. Joffre held some commanders responsible: at the end of August he removed, among others, General Ruffey. A few days later the Fourth Army put up stubborn resistance to the Germans in the [Battle of the Meuse](/bitwy/bitwa-nad-moza).

## Significance

The Battle of the Ardennes exposed the weaknesses of pre-war French doctrine, which relied on attack at all costs, as well as failures of reconnaissance. After this experience the French army began to change its tactics and uniforms. In 1915 soldiers were issued less conspicuous horizon-blue uniforms.
