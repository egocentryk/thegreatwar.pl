On 4 August 1914 at 8.02 a.m. German troops crossed the Belgian border at the village of [Gemmenich](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemmenich), near the point where the borders of Belgium, Germany and the Netherlands meet. The first Belgians to encounter the invader were two border guards approached by a detachment of uhlans. At the same time German columns set off towards the [Meuse](/miejsca/moza) near [Visé](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visé), where the Belgians had managed to blow up the bridge.

## The fortress of Liège

The Germans' first objective was [Liège](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liège). The city lay in the Meuse valley, on the main road leading from Germany into the interior of Belgium, between the Dutch border and the forests of the Ardennes. It was surrounded by a ring of twelve forts, built at the end of the 19th century a few kilometres from the centre. The [fortress of Liège](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twierdza_Liège) was considered one of the strongest in Europe. Its defence was commanded by General Gérard Leman.

Six brigades under General [Otto von Emmich](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Emmich) were assigned to take Liège. The plan assumed that the surprised Belgians would be unable to defend the forts effectively and that the city would fall within a few days, opening the way for the main forces of the German army.

## The course of the fighting

On the night of 5/6 August the Germans attacked between the forts, suffering heavy losses. One of the brigades, however, broke through the defensive line. When its commander was killed, command was taken over by a staff officer, [Erich Ludendorff](/postacie/erich-ludendorff). On 7 August his soldiers entered the city and occupied the citadel. The success made Ludendorff a hero, and later one of the most important German commanders.

The forts, however, held out. The Germans brought up heavy siege mortars, including Krupp's famous ["Big Berthas"](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-Gerät) and Austro-Hungarian Škoda mortars. From 12 August their shells smashed one concrete fortification after another. On 15 August an explosion at Fort Loncin buried part of the garrison, and the badly injured General Leman was taken prisoner. On 16 August the last forts fell.

## Significance

The [Battle of Liège](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Liège) was the first great battle on the Western Front. Belgian resistance delayed the march of the German armies by several days and gave time to France and Britain, although historians disagree about how much this mattered. The defence of Liège became a symbol of Belgian courage. At the same time, from the very first days of the invasion German troops committed crimes against the civilian population, including at Visé, which foreshadowed the tragic fate of occupied Belgium.
