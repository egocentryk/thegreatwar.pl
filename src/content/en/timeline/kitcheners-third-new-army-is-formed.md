On 13 September 1914 the British War Office issued Army Order No. 388, which created the third of the so-called New Armies. Like the two before it, it was to consist of six infantry divisions formed from volunteers.

The Secretary of State for War, [Lord Herbert Kitchener](/postacie/horatio-kitchener), had foreseen a long war from the start and the need to raise an army of millions. There was no shortage of volunteers. By 12 September almost half a million men had joined up, more than could be absorbed by the [first New Army](/pierwsza-nowa-armia-kitchenera), formed in August, and the second, created two days earlier. The third New Army, known as "K3", comprised the divisions numbered 21 to 26. Its battalions were formed as part of existing regiments, and the recruits were gathered in camps in southern England, among others on [Salisbury Plain](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_Plain). They lacked everything: uniforms, rifles, billets and instructors, and many lived in tents for the first weeks.

The K3 divisions went into action in 1915. The 21st and 24th Divisions, newly arrived in France, suffered heavy losses at the [Battle of Loos](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Loos) in September 1915. The 22nd and 26th Divisions went in the autumn of 1915 to the [Salonika front](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_salonicki), where they remained until the end of the war. The 23rd and 25th Divisions fought on the Western Front, and from the end of 1917 the 23rd Division also fought in Italy.
