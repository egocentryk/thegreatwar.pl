On 19 September 1914 the first units of Indian Expeditionary Force A set out from [Egypt](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egipt) for France. They were brigades of the Lahore Division, which a few days earlier, after a long voyage from India, [had arrived at Suez](/korpus-indyjski-przybywa-do-suezu).

The stay in Egypt was short. The soldiers sailed through the [Suez Canal](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanał_Sueski) or went by rail to the Mediterranean ports, mainly [Alexandria](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandria), where they were re-embarked on transports. The convoy, escorted by warships, sailed across the [Mediterranean](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morze_Śródziemne) to France. For the time being only the Sirhind Brigade remained in Egypt, guarding the canal.

On 26 September the first transports put in at [Marseille](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsylia). The inhabitants greeted the sepoys and Gurkhas with tremendous enthusiasm, because their arrival meant long-awaited reinforcements for France. In October they were joined by the Meerut Division, and the two formations made up the Indian Corps, which went into action in Flanders that same autumn.
