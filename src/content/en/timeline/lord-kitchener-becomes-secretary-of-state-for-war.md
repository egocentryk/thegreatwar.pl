On 6 August 1914 Field Marshal [Lord Kitchener](/postacie/horatio-kitchener) took office as British Secretary of State for War. He had been appointed the day before, and now replaced at the War Office the prime minister, [H. H. Asquith](/postacie/herbert-henry-asquith), who had headed the department since 30 March 1914.

## The prime minister at the War Office

Asquith had taken over the War Office in the spring of 1914 after the resignation of [John Seely](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Seely). Seely had had to go after the so-called Curragh incident, when a number of officers stationed in Ireland declared that they would not obey an order to use force against the Ulster Protestants who opposed Irish Home Rule. In peacetime the prime minister could combine both posts. Once war broke out this became impossible, and the press and public opinion demanded that Kitchener should head the department.

## Hero of the empire

Kitchener was Britain's most famous soldier. He had made his name with the victory at the [Battle of Omdurman](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Omdurmanem) in the Sudan in 1898, held command in the [Boer War](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/II_wojna_burska) and later commanded the army in India. When war broke out he happened to be in England on leave from Egypt, where he served as British Consul-General. A serving officer in the cabinet was a rare exception in the British political tradition.

## A war lasting years

Kitchener at once stood out for his sober assessment of the situation. Most politicians and soldiers expected a short war, over by Christmas. The new minister predicted that it would last at least three years and that Britain would need an army of millions. As early as 7 August he issued an appeal for the first hundred thousand volunteers. Later rounds of recruitment produced the so-called New Armies, and his image on the poster with the slogan "Lord Kitchener Wants You" became one of the symbols of the war.

## Significance

Kitchener headed the War Office until June 1916. Over time his position weakened, especially after the shell crisis of 1915 and the failure of the Dardanelles operation. He was killed on 5 June 1916, when the cruiser [HMS Hampshire](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Hampshire_(1903)), in which he was sailing on a mission to Russia, struck a mine and sank off the Orkneys. His greatest achievement remained the building of a mass volunteer army, which in the following years fought on every front.
