On 13 July 1918 General [Józef Haller](/postacie/jozef-haller), commander of the II Polish Corps, which had been destroyed two months earlier [at Kaniów](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-kaniowem), reached [Le Havre](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawr). Behind him lay a journey of several weeks through Russia in the grip of civil war and a voyage across the northern seas. He had sailed from [Murmansk](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmańsk) on 3 July, calling at Scotland on the way. This date of his arrival in France is given by the notes to the post-war edition of [Roman Dmowski](/postacie/roman-dmowski)'s *Polityka polska i odbudowanie państwa* and by biographies of the general. The date of his departure from Murmansk is also confirmed by Henryk Bagiński's work on the Polish army in the East, published in 1921.

Haller managed to slip out of the encirclement at Kaniów in the confusion after the corps capitulated. He made his way through Kiev to Moscow, where he arrived clandestinely on 12 June. There he came to an understanding with the [Polish Council of Inter-Party Union](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rada_Polska_Zjednoczenia_Międzypartyjnego) and helped organise a secret military command for the scattered soldiers of the former Polish corps. According to some accounts, in Moscow he received word that the [Polish National Committee](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komitet_Narodowy_Polski_(1917–1919)) in Paris saw him as commander of the army being formed in France. He chose the route through Murmansk, from which it was easiest to reach the Entente countries. Other Polish soldiers took the same route. In the far north of Russia Polish units began to form, which later fought alongside the Allies.

From Le Havre the general went on to Paris. Dmowski recalled that Haller appeared at the offices of the National Committee the day after arriving in the capital, and judged that he "had arrived just in time". On 17 July the committee unanimously co-opted him as a member. The [Polish Army in France](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armia_Polska_we_Francji) then consisted of one division in the process of formation, commanded by a French general, and its oldest regiment was already fighting in Champagne. Only three weeks earlier its regiments had [received their colours](/sztandary-dla-armii-polskiej-we-francji). The committee was looking for a Polish commander for it, and the fame of Rarańcza and Kaniów meant that the choice fell on Haller. On 4 October 1918 the committee formally entrusted him with command of the army, which came to be known after him as Haller's Army.
