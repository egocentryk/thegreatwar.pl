On 19 September 1914 French warships shelled the fortifications of the Austro-Hungarian naval base at [Kotor](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotor), known in Italian as Cattaro. The base lay deep inside the [Bay of Kotor](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zatoka_Kotorska), at the southern end of the Austro-Hungarian coast, right by the border with Montenegro.

## A sortie into the Adriatic

Since August the Allied fleet in the Mediterranean had been commanded by the French Vice-Admiral Augustin Boué de Lapeyrère. His battleships had already sunk the [cruiser *Zenta*](/zatopienie-krazownika-zenta) and on 1 September had for the first time shelled the forts at the entrance to the Bay of Kotor. The French hoped in this way to provoke the Austro-Hungarian fleet into leaving port and giving battle.

On 18 and 19 September the French fleet again entered the [Adriatic](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morze_Adriatyckie), reaching as far north as the island of [Vis](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vis_(wyspa)), then called Lissa. On the way back several French cruisers came within range of Kotor's coastal guns. The battleships *Patrie* and *Démocratie* then opened fire on the batteries to silence them and cover the withdrawing cruisers. Once again the Austro-Hungarian fleet did not put to sea.

## Guns on Lovćen

At the same time the French tried to attack Kotor from the land side. On 18 and 19 September a detachment of naval artillery with eight guns was landed at the Montenegrin port of [Bar](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_(Czarnogóra)). With great difficulty the guns were hauled up the slopes of Mount Lovćen, which rises above the bay. On 19 October they opened fire on the Austro-Hungarian positions. Austria-Hungary responded by bringing the battleship [SMS *Radetzky*](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Radetzky_(1909)) into the bay, and her heavy guns quickly gained the upper hand. After a few weeks the French withdrew their surviving guns, and Kotor remained in Austro-Hungarian hands until the end of the war.
