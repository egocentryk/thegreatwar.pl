On 29 September 1914, according to the British chronicle of the war, the Germans abandoned their offensive on the [Niemen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niemen). The German [Eighth Army](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_Armia_(Cesarstwo_Niemieckie)) of General [Richard von Schubert](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_von_Schubert), which [a few days earlier](/poczatek-walk-nad-niemnem) had tried to cross to the east bank of the river, began to withdraw towards the border of East Prussia.

## Failed crossings

After the Battle of the Masurian Lakes the Germans entered Russian territory and occupied part of the Suwałki Governorate. At the end of September their units tried to force the Niemen in the area of [Druskieniki](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druskieniki) and Sopoćkinie near Grodno, and further north, between Olita and Kovno. On the river, however, the Russians had the new [10th Army](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_Armia_(Imperium_Rosyjskie)) of General Vasily Flug, clearly more numerous than Schubert's weakened army. Most of the German forces had earlier been sent to Silesia, to the new Ninth Army, which was preparing for an offensive in the Kingdom of Poland. The crossings failed, and the units that had reached the other bank were thrown back.

## Counter-attack in the Augustów Forest

The decisive factor proved to be a Russian manoeuvre on the southern wing. The corps of the 10th Army advanced through the [Augustów Forest](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puszcza_Augustowska), threatening to cut off the German units standing on the Niemen. According to some studies, the command of the Eighth Army had already ordered a retreat from the bend of the river towards Suwałki on 26 September. The Russians retook [Augustów](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustów) on 27 or 28 September after heavy fighting in the forests, and at the beginning of October entered [Suwałki](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwałki). In the literature this stage of the fighting is called the Battle of Augustów or the first Augustów operation. Its dates are given differently, most often from the end of September to 3–5 October.

In mid-October the Russians again stood on the border of East Prussia and entered the eastern edges of the province. Schubert lost his command on 8 October. The Suwałki region remained in Russian hands until February 1915, when the Germans destroyed the 10th Army in the Second Battle of the Masurian Lakes, and the fighting once more swept through the Augustów Forest.
