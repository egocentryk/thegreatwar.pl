In the early afternoon of 31 July 1914 Kaiser [Wilhelm II](/postacie/wilhelm-ii) proclaimed a "state of imminent danger of war" (Zustand drohender Kriegsgefahr) in [Germany](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarstwo_Niemieckie). It was a direct response to the news of general mobilisation in Russia, which had reached Berlin before noon.

The state of imminent danger of war was not yet mobilisation, but it was the last stage before it. It introduced [martial law](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_wojenny) throughout the Reich: executive power in the individual districts passed into the hands of military commanders. The exception was the [Kingdom of Bavaria](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Królestwo_Bawarii), which retained separate powers in this matter and introduced similar measures on its own authority. Freedom of the press and of communications was restricted, protection of the borders, railway lines and important installations was stepped up, and the army began preparations for mobilisation.

For the German General Staff, headed by [Helmuth von Moltke](/postacie/helmuth-von-moltke), every day counted. The war plan relied on a swift blow against France before Russia could fully mobilise its army. The military therefore pressed for the next steps to be taken without delay. At the same time as proclaiming the state of imminent danger of war, the government sent an ultimatum to Russia and an inquiry to France.

In the evening Wilhelm II addressed the crowds gathered below the balcony of the palace in [Berlin](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin). He said that a grave hour had come upon Germany and that the sword was being forced into its hand. He presented Germany as a state compelled to defend itself, which was meant to convince the public that the war, if it broke out, would be a defensive one.

The state of imminent danger of war lasted less than a day. On the afternoon of 1 August, after the ultimatum had expired, the Kaiser signed the order for general mobilisation, and in the evening Germany declared war on Russia.
