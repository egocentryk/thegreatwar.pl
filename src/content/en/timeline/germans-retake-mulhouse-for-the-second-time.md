On 25 August 1914 German troops occupied [Mulhouse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miluza) in [Alsace](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzacja) for the second time. This time there was no fighting. The French Army of Alsace, which had entered the city on 19 August (see [French retake Mulhouse](/francuzi-ponownie-zajmuja-miluze)), withdrew from it on the orders of the high command.

## The order to retreat

The fate of Mulhouse was decided by events on other sectors of the front. After the French defeats in Lorraine, the Ardennes and at Charleroi, the Commander-in-Chief, [Joseph Joffre](/postacie/joseph-joffre), needed every division to defend northern France. As early as 22 August part of the Army of Alsace's forces was handed over to the neighbouring First Army, and on 24 August the main body of the VII Corps and one reserve division began to be moved by rail to the west, to the Somme area. There they became part of the Sixth Army, then being formed.

The weakened Army of Alsace of General [Paul Pau](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pau) could not hold the ground it had gained. The French abandoned Mulhouse, as well as [Altkirch](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altkirch) and most of the southern Alsace they had occupied earlier, and withdrew to a line covering [Belfort](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfort). Sources give different dates for the evacuation of the city, from 24 to 26 August. On 28 August the Army of Alsace was disbanded.

## The end of hope

So ended, for the second time in a month, the French attempt to regain Mulhouse. The first time, the Germans had retaken the city as early as 10 August (see [Germans retake Mulhouse](/niemcy-odbijaja-miluze)). This time Mulhouse remained in German hands until the end of the war. The French returned to it only in November 1918, after the armistice.
