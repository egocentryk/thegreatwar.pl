A British chronology of the war gives under 8 October 1914 the end of the first Russian invasion of Hungary. Two weeks earlier Russian units had [crossed the Carpathian passes](/rosjanie-wkraczaja-na-wegry) and descended into the valleys on the Hungarian side of the mountains. At the beginning of October the defenders went over to the counter-attack, and after the [recapture of Máramarossziget](/austriacy-odbijaja-syhot) the Russians were falling back along the whole sector, towards the crest of the mountains and back into Galicia.

The western part of the threatened belt was recovered first. At the [Uzsok Pass](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przełęcz_Użocka) the Russians had advanced at the end of September down the Uzh valley as far as Fenyvesvölgy (today Stavne) before they were stopped. By 5 October, after heavy fighting, the pass was retaken by units of General Karl Tersztyánszky's IV Corps, which then went on north towards Turka in Galicia. Further east, in Bereg County, the defenders of the Verecke and Beskid passes had withdrawn on 30 September to [Szolyva](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swalawa). Reinforcements arrived there, march battalions and Honvéd batteries, and General Hofmann's group was able to move forward. According to the 1922 Encyclopaedia Britannica, on 8 October his corps crossed the Beskid Pass back to the Galician side of the mountains and advanced on Stryi.

The fighting did not end there, however. In Máramaros General Attems's troops pursued the Russians up the Tisza valley and in the following days were still fighting their rearguards around [Kőrösmező](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasina) (today Yasinia), below the Tatar Pass. Units of the [Polish Legions](/tematy/polskie-formacje-wojskowe) also fought in this area; they followed the retreating Russians across the mountains and on 12 October took Rafajłowa on the Galician side. Skirmishes near the pass thus continued for a good part of October.

The expedition brought the Russians no lasting gains, but it shook Hungary. For several weeks thousands of people fled from the border counties, and cholera was spreading in the region. The impression that the enemy could cross the Carpathians and threaten the Hungarian Plain remained for a long time. The Russians returned to the passes as early as November, and the fighting in the Carpathians then went on all winter.
