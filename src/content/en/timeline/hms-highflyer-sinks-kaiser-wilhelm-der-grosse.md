On 26 August 1914 the British cruiser HMS *Highflyer* surprised the German [auxiliary cruiser](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krążownik_pomocniczy) [*Kaiser Wilhelm der Grosse*](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Kaiser_Wilhelm_der_Große) off the coast of [Río de Oro](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Oro), a Spanish colony on the Atlantic coast of Africa. After an exchange of fire lasting more than an hour, the former luxury liner went to the bottom. She was one of the first German raiders lost in this war.

## A three-week raid

*Kaiser Wilhelm der Grosse*, a Norddeutscher Lloyd liner converted into a warship, [sailed from Bremen on 4 August](/krazownik-pomocniczy-kaiser-wilhelm-der-grosse-wyplywa-na-atlantyk). Commanded by Captain Max Reymann, she sank three British ships in the Atlantic, *Tubal Cain*, *Kaipara* and *Nyanza*, having first taken their crews on board. Two liners she encountered with women and children on board, *Galician* and *Arlanza*, she let go.

At the end of August the raider, running short of coal, put into a bay near Villa Cisneros, today's [Dakhla](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad-Dachla) in [Western Sahara](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_Zachodnia). German and Austro-Hungarian supply ships were waiting for her there. Spain was neutral, and international law allowed the warships of belligerents to stay in neutral ports only briefly. *Kaiser Wilhelm der Grosse*, however, had already been there for more than a week.

## The clash

On 26 August HMS *Highflyer*, an old protected cruiser of 1898 commanded by Captain Henry Buller, appeared in the bay. The British captain called on the Germans to surrender. Reymann replied that he was in neutral Spanish waters and that an attack would violate them. Buller took the view that the Germans had been the first to break Spanish neutrality by prolonging their stay, and at 3.10 p.m. he opened fire. The fight lasted until 4.45 p.m. On the British side one sailor was killed and several were wounded.

## Sunk or scuttled?

The accounts of the two sides differ. The British announced that the German ship had been sunk by their shells. According to the Germans, *Kaiser Wilhelm der Grosse* had run out of ammunition, and Reymann, unwilling to surrender his ship, had her scuttled with explosive charges prepared in advance. Modern accounts usually combine the two versions: the ship was damaged by British fire but was finally sent to the bottom by her own crew. On this basis a British prize court in 1916 awarded *Highflyer*'s crew a bounty for destroying an enemy warship.

Most of the Germans managed to get ashore before the ship sank. Sources describe the fate of the crew in different ways, and Reymann himself eventually made his way back to Germany. The wreck in shallow water remained visible for decades. The loss of *Kaiser Wilhelm der Grosse* showed that big liners, which consumed enormous quantities of coal, were poorly suited to raiding: they depended on supply ships and neutral ports, where they were easy to track down.
