When Germany declared war on Russia on 1 August 1914, the border between the two empires became a front line. It ran through the lands of the old Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, separating the [Prussian partition](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabór_pruski) from the [Kingdom of Poland](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Królestwo_Polskie_(kongresowe)), which was subject to Russia. On the [Eastern Front](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_wschodni_(I_wojna_światowa)), therefore, the war began on Polish soil.

On the very first day shots were exchanged on the border between [East Prussia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prusy_Wschodnie) and the Kingdom of Poland. According to local accounts, at [Prostken](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostki), on the border opposite [Grajewo](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grajewo), Russian cavalry fired on a train carrying soldiers of a Prussian infantry regiment. These were among the first shots of the German-Russian war.

Russian war plans did not provide for the defence of the western part of the Kingdom of Poland, which jutted far out between Prussia and Austria-Hungary. The Tsar's troops therefore withdrew from the border towns, and the Germans occupied them almost without a fight. On 2 August a battalion of a Prussian infantry regiment entered [Kalisz](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalisz), which the Russians had abandoned, and on 3 August the first German soldiers appeared in [Częstochowa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Częstochowa).

For Kalisz the arrival of the Germans proved tragic. From 4 August the town suffered artillery bombardment, executions and arson. Within a few weeks almost the entire historic centre had been destroyed and most of the inhabitants had fled. The [destruction of Kalisz](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zburzenie_Kalisza) became one of the first and most shocking examples of violence against civilians in the Great War.

For Poles the war had a particularly dramatic dimension. The inhabitants of the three partitions served in the armies of Russia, Germany and Austria-Hungary, and so on opposite sides of the front. At the same time, a war between the partitioning powers offered hope of regaining independence. As early as 6 August the [First Cadre Company](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierwsza_Kompania_Kadrowa), formed by [Józef Piłsudski](/postacie/jozef-pilsudski), set out from Kraków towards the Kingdom of Poland.
