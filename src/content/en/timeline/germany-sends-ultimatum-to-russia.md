On 31 July 1914, a few hours after receiving news of general mobilisation in Russia, the government of [Germany](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarstwo_Niemieckie) sent an ultimatum to St Petersburg. At about 3:30 p.m. the German ambassador in [St Petersburg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersburg), Count Friedrich von Pourtalès, was instructed to demand that [Russia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Rosyjskie) halt, within twelve hours, all war preparations directed against Germany and Austria-Hungary. If Russia did not agree to this demand, Germany would declare [mobilisation](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilizacja).

Pourtalès presented the ultimatum to the Foreign Minister, [Sergey Sazonov](/postacie/siergiej-sazonow), late in the evening – according to some accounts at 11 p.m., according to others around midnight on the night of 31 July to 1 August. The deadline expired at noon on 1 August. In practice Berlin left Russia no choice: halting a mobilisation already under way was unacceptable to St Petersburg, and German mobilisation meant war.

On the same day Germany also approached [France](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/III_Republika_Francuska). The ambassador in Paris was to ask whether France would remain neutral in a German-Russian war, and to demand a reply within eighteen hours. His secret instructions provided that if France declared neutrality, he would further demand that the fortresses of [Toul](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toul) and [Verdun](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdun_(Moza)) be handed over to Germany for the duration of the war as a pledge. It never came to that: the Prime Minister, [René Viviani](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Viviani), replied on 1 August that France would act in accordance with its interests.

Russia left the ultimatum unanswered. On the afternoon of 1 August Germany declared mobilisation, and in the evening Pourtalès handed Sazonov the [declaration of war](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wypowiedzenie_wojny). The ultimatum of 31 July was thus the last step before war between Germany and Russia, and the question put to Paris signalled that the war would not be confined to eastern Europe.
