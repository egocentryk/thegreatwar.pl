On 20 August 1914 in [East Prussia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prusy_Wschodnie), near the town of Gumbinnen, in Polish Gąbin, the first major battle on the Eastern Front took place. The chronicles of the time give the dates 19 and 20 August, because the first clashes preceded the main battle. General [Paul von Rennenkampf](/postacie/paul-von-rennenkampf)'s Russian First Army repulsed an attack by the German Eighth Army.

## Russia keeps its word

Russia had promised France that it would strike at Germany as soon as possible, even before mobilisation was complete. In mid-August its armies entered East Prussia. The Germans, concentrating on the offensive against France, defended the province with only one army. Its commander, General Maximilian von Prittwitz, decided to crush Rennenkampf before the second Russian army, under General Samsonov, arrived from the south.

## A failed attack

On the morning of 20 August the Germans attacked. On the northern flank they were successful, but in the centre the attack by General [August von Mackensen](/postacie/august-von-mackensen)'s corps collapsed under Russian artillery fire. Both sides suffered heavy losses. The battlefield remained in Russian hands, and Prittwitz, alarmed by the news of Samsonov's army approaching, considered withdrawing as far as the Vistula.

## New commanders

The German Supreme Army Command refused to give up East Prussia. On 22 August Prittwitz was dismissed, and in his place came General [Paul von Hindenburg](/postacie/paul-von-hindenburg) and Erich Ludendorff. A few days later they destroyed Samsonov's army at the [Battle of Tannenberg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Tannenbergiem).

For Polish readers it is worth recalling that the fighting in East Prussia took place in areas closely bound up with Polish history: in Masuria and near the border of the Kingdom of Poland. Poles also fought in the armies of both sides.
