On 2 September 1914 the Battle of Komarów in the [Zamość region](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamojszczyzna) came to an end. On the night of 1/2 September the Russian Fifth Army of General [Pavel Plehve](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paweł_Plehwe), threatened with encirclement, broke contact with the enemy and withdrew north-east towards the [Bug](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug). The battlefield remained in the hands of the Austro-Hungarian Fourth Army of General [Moritz von Auffenberg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Auffenberg).

## A trap that did not close

The fighting had been going on since [26 August](/poczatek-bitwy-pod-komarowem). After several days of see-saw engagements the wings of Auffenberg's army began to outflank the Russians on both sides. Plehve lost [Komarów](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komarów-Osada), and his corps were pushed back towards the river [Huczwa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huczwa). The Austrians hoped to repeat the German success at Tannenberg, where Samsonov's army had been destroyed a few days earlier.

That did not happen, however. The commanders of the Austro-Hungarian wings, alarmed by reports of Russian forces on their own flanks, halted their advance. The Fifth Army managed to withdraw through the gap between them. It lost many soldiers, prisoners and guns, but escaped destruction. Figures for the losses vary widely: according to various estimates between 10,000 and 20,000 Russians were taken prisoner, and the Austro-Hungarians also suffered heavy losses.

## Victory without a decision

Komarów was, together with Kraśnik, the greatest success of the Austro-Hungarian army in the first weeks of the war. Auffenberg became a hero of the monarchy, and in 1915 he received the title of baron with the predicate "von Komarów". The victory did not, however, change the course of the campaign. In eastern Galicia the Russians defeated the weaker Austro-Hungarian armies [on the Złota Lipa and Gniła Lipa](/koniec-bitwy-nad-zlota-lipa) and were approaching Lviv. Conrad immediately turned Auffenberg's tired troops south, towards [Rawa Ruska](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawa_Ruska), to save the capital of Galicia.

## War in the Zamość region

The battle was fought on the territory of the Kingdom of Poland, between [Zamość](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamość), Komarów and [Tomaszów Lubelski](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomaszów_Lubelski). For several days the front rolled through Polish villages and small towns, and their inhabitants suffered from shelling, fires and requisitions. Many Poles fought in the Austro-Hungarian and Russian ranks, often facing each other. The fallen left behind numerous war cemeteries, which can still be found around Tomaszów Lubelski and Komarów today.
