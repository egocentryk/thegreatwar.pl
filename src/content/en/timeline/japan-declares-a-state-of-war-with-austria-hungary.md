On 25 August 1914 [Japan](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarstwo_Wielkiej_Japonii) announced that it was in a state of war with [Austria-Hungary](/kraje/austro-wegry). Two states at opposite ends of Eurasia, with no disputes between them, became enemies because of a single ship anchored in a Chinese port.

## A cruiser at Tsingtao

In the summer of 1914 the Austro-Hungarian protected cruiser [SMS Kaiserin Elisabeth](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Kaiserin_Elisabeth) was serving in Far Eastern waters. At the end of July she put in at Tsingtao, today's [Qingdao](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao), the main port of the German leased territory of [Kiautschou](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou) in China. When war broke out, the ship was too weak and too slow to fight her way home.

Vienna at first wanted to avoid a conflict with Japan. In mid-August it ordered the cruiser to be disarmed and her crew to be taken to the Austro-Hungarian concession in [Tientsin](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiencin). The sailors had already left by train when a new order arrived: the ship was after all to take part in the defence of Tsingtao together with the Germans. The crew returned to the port, travelling in civilian clothes so as not to violate China's neutrality.

## Who declared war on whom?

Sources describe what happened on 24 and 25 August in different ways. According to a British chronology of the war, on 24 August Austria-Hungary broke off diplomatic relations with Japan (see [Austria-Hungary breaks off relations with Japan](/austro-wegry-zrywaja-stosunki-z-japonia)), and on 25 August Japan broke off relations with Austria-Hungary and announced a state of war. Most modern studies say simply that on 25 August Japan declared war on Austria-Hungary because Vienna refused to withdraw the cruiser from Tsingtao. In some lists, however, the same step is presented as a declaration of war on Japan by Austria-Hungary. All sources agree on the date: from 25 August the two states were at war.

## The defence of Tsingtao

At the end of August the Japanese fleet began the blockade of Tsingtao. At the start of the siege the Kaiserin Elisabeth sortied together with the German gunboat Jaguar against the Japanese ships, and later some of her guns were removed and set up on land as a battery defending the fortress. During the [siege of Tsingtao](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblężenie_Tsingtau) the cruiser shelled Japanese positions until her ammunition ran out. On 2 November 1914, a few days before the fortress capitulated, the crew scuttled the ship. The Austro-Hungarian sailors went into Japanese captivity together with the Germans, returning home only in 1920.

## Significance

The state of war between Japan and Austria-Hungary was mainly of symbolic importance. The Habsburg monarchy had no possessions or interests in Asia that Japan could threaten, and its only force in the Far East was an old cruiser. It shows, however, how the alliance system drew more and more states into the war. A few weeks earlier the war had been an Austro-Serbian conflict, and by the end of August it already embraced Europe, Africa and East Asia.
