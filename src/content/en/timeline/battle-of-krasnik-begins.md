On 23 August 1914, south of [Kraśnik](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraśnik), in Congress Poland, the first great battle between the armies of Austria-Hungary and Russia began. The [Battle of Kraśnik](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Kraśnikiem) opened the series of clashes known as the [Battle of Galicia](/bitwy/bitwa-galicyjska).

## Two armies meet

The Austro-Hungarian chief of staff, Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf, decided to strike north from Galicia, between the Vistula and the Bug. On the left wing advanced the First Army under General Viktor Dankl. Marching south to meet it was the Russian Fourth Army under General Anton Salza. Neither side had properly reconnoitred the other's position, and they met almost by chance.

## The first Austro-Hungarian victory

The fighting lasted three days. The Austro-Hungarians, who had numerical superiority, pushed back the Russian right wing, and on 25 August forced the whole Russian army to retreat towards [Lublin](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin). It was the Austro-Hungarian army's first victory of the war, especially valuable after the defeat in Serbia. Salza lost his command.

## War on Polish soil

The Battle of Kraśnik is a reminder that the great battles of the Eastern Front were fought above all on Polish soil. Tens of thousands of Poles served in the armies of both partitioning powers, and they often found themselves facing one another. The war devastated Polish towns and villages, and the civilian population suffered from requisitions, evacuations and the passage of armies.
