On 3 August 1914 the [Kingdom of Italy](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Królestwo_Włoch_(1861–1946)) declared its neutrality in the war that was just engulfing Europe. The decision had been taken the day before by the government of Prime Minister [Antonio Salandra](/postacie/antonio-salandra) and Foreign Minister Antonino di San Giuliano. It was a blow to Germany and [Austria-Hungary](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Węgry), with which Italy had formed the [Triple Alliance](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trójprzymierze) since 1882.

The government in Rome justified its decision by the provisions of the treaty itself. The Triple Alliance was a defensive alliance: it obliged the signatories to give help only if one of them was attacked. Yet it was Austria-Hungary that had presented Serbia with an ultimatum and declared war on it, and Germany that had declared war on Russia and France. In Rome's judgement the Central Powers were the attacking side, so the so-called casus foederis, the situation obliging participation in the war, had not arisen.

The Italians had a second argument too. The treaty provided that Austria-Hungary would agree with Italy on any action in the Balkans that might upset the balance in the region. Vienna, however, had not informed Rome in advance of the ultimatum to Serbia. The Italian authorities regarded this as a breach of its obligations as an ally.

Behind the official arguments lay deeper reasons. Italy had long been a rival of Austria-Hungary, and many Italian circles demanded the annexation of Trentino and Trieste, largely inhabited by Italians but belonging to the Habsburg monarchy. The country was also unprepared for a great war, and its long coastlines would have been defenceless against the British and French fleets.

Neutrality was not Rome's last word. In the following months Italy negotiated with both sides, and in April 1915 it concluded the secret [Treaty of London](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traktat_londyński_(1915)) with the Entente. On 23 May 1915 it declared war on Austria-Hungary, its former ally.
