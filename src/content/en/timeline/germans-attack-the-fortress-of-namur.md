On 21 August 1914 German troops began their attack on the [fortress of Namur](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twierdza_Namur). After the fall of Liège, it was the last great fortress on the Germans' route through the Meuse valley into France.

## A fortress where rivers meet

[Namur](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namur) lies where the [Sambre](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambra) flows into the [Meuse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moza). At the end of the 19th century it was surrounded by a ring of nine forts, designed, like the forts of Liège, by General Henri Brialmont. The fortress was defended by the Belgian 4th Division together with the fort garrisons, some 37,000 soldiers in all under the command of General Michel.

On the other side stood the group of General [Max von Gallwitz](/postacie/max-von-gallwitz), part of Karl von Bülow's 2nd Army. The Germans had learned the lessons of Liège, where the forts had long resisted the infantry. This time they brought up the heaviest artillery straight away: German 420 mm mortars, the so-called [Big Berthas](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-Gerät), and batteries of Austro-Hungarian 305 mm Škoda mortars.

## The forts under fire

The bombardment began on 21 August. The heavy shells pierced the concrete vaults of the forts, destroyed the armoured turrets and buried the garrisons in their underground chambers. The forts, built with much lighter artillery in mind, stood no chance. Nor did the nearness of the French help the Belgians: General Lanrezac's army was at that time fighting hard on the Sambre, in the Battle of Charleroi, and could give the fortress no real support.

On 23 August General Michel concluded that further defence of the town was impossible and led the 4th Division out to the south. On the same day the Germans entered Namur. The last forts capitulated on 25 August.

## Significance

Namur fell much faster than Liège. This showed that fortresses built in the 19th century could not withstand modern siege artillery. Some of the defenders, however, managed to avoid captivity. About 12,000 soldiers of the 4th Division made their way through France and returned to Belgium by sea to join the army defending [Antwerp](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerpia).
