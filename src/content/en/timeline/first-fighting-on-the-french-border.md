On 2 August 1914, the day before Germany formally declared war on France, the first clashes took place on the Franco-German border. The best known of them occurred in the village of [Joncherey](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joncherey), near [Belfort](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfort), on the border with [Alsace](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzacja), then German.

In the morning a small German cavalry patrol under Lieutenant Albert Mayer crossed the border and ventured deep into French territory. At about 10 a.m. at Joncherey it came upon a French post commanded by Corporal Jules-André Peugeot. The Frenchman called on the Germans to surrender. Mayer drew his pistol and fired, mortally wounding Peugeot. The French soldiers opened fire and killed Mayer.

Jules-André Peugeot, a 21-year-old teacher doing his military service, died shortly after the clash. He is regarded as the first French soldier killed in the First World War. Albert Mayer was the first German soldier killed on the [Western Front](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_zachodni_(I_wojna_światowa)). Both died before war had formally broken out between their countries.

The clash at Joncherey also had a political dimension. Since 30 July France had kept its troops 10 kilometres back from the border so as not to give Germany a pretext for accusations of aggression. The entry of a German patrol into French territory allowed Paris to present itself as the party attacked. Other border incidents also took place that same day, and the Germans in turn accused the French of violating their border and of aircraft overflights.

A day later, on 3 August, Germany declared war on [France](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/III_Republika_Francuska). Today a monument to Corporal Peugeot stands in Joncherey, and the place where he died is a symbol of the beginning of the war in the west.
