On 6 August 1914 [Serbia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Królestwo_Serbii) declared war on [Germany](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarstwo_Niemieckie). The decision was taken by the government of Prime Minister [Nikola Pašić](/postacie/nikola-pasic), working in the country's wartime capital, [Niš](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nisz). A week earlier Serbia had been attacked by [Austria-Hungary](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Węgry), and Germany had stood behind Vienna's policy since the start of the crisis.

## A declaration without a common border

Serbia and Germany had no common border, and Germany had not previously declared war on Serbia. From a military point of view the declaration changed little: Serbia was fighting the Austro-Hungarian army on its borders along the Drina, the Sava and the Danube. It did, however, carry a clear political message. Belgrade was showing that it regarded Germany as jointly responsible for the war, since it was German support, expressed as early as the beginning of July, that had allowed Austria-Hungary to take a hard line towards Serbia.

The declaration of war also placed Serbia unambiguously on the side of the Entente. Within a few days Germany found itself at war with Russia, France and Great Britain, and Serbia joined the camp of its enemies as a full participant in the European war, not merely the victim of a local conflict.

## The first fighting

In August 1914 Serbia was threatened only by the Austro-Hungarian army. On 12 August Austro-Hungarian troops crossed the Drina and began the invasion. A few days later the Serbs, commanded by Vojvoda Radomir Putnik, won the first Allied victory of the war at the Battle of Cer and drove out the invaders. Further Austro-Hungarian offensives in 1914 also failed, and that year's [Serbian campaign](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampania_serbska_(I_wojna_światowa)) was a string of defeats for Vienna.

## Consequences

Germany dealt with Serbia only in 1915. In the autumn of that year combined German, Austro-Hungarian and Bulgarian forces under Field Marshal [August von Mackensen](/postacie/august-von-mackensen) struck Serbia from several directions. The country was overrun, and the remnants of the Serbian army, together with the government and the king, withdrew through the mountains of Albania to the Adriatic. The declaration of 6 August 1914 thus foreshadowed a clash that came late, but had tragic consequences for Serbia.
