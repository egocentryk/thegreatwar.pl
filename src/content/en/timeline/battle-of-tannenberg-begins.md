On 23 August 1914, in the south of [East Prussia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prusy_Wschodnie), fighting began that went down in history as the [Battle of Tannenberg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Tannenbergiem). That day the corps of the Russian Second Army of General [Alexander Samsonov](/postacie/aleksandr-samsonow), marching north from the Kingdom of Poland, clashed with the German XX Corps near [Neidenburg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidzica).

## First clashes

The German XX Corps of General Friedrich von Scholtz was defending the southern border of East Prussia. On 23 and 24 August its units fought the Russian XV Corps and then withdrew to new positions. Samsonov concluded that the Germans were in retreat and drove his exhausted troops further north. He did not know that the enemy was preparing a trap.

That same day the new commanders, Paul von Hindenburg and Erich Ludendorff, arrived at the headquarters of the German Eighth Army. They adopted a plan to take most of the forces away from Gumbinnen, where Rennenkampf's army stood, and throw them against Samsonov.

## Dates of the battle

Chronologies of the war often give 23 August as the start of the Battle of Tannenberg, because the first clashes took place that day. Most historians, however, regard the battle proper as the fighting of 26–30 August, when the Germans struck the wings of the Russian army and closed it in an encirclement.

The fighting took place in what is today Warmia and Masuria, around [Hohenstein](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztynek), Neidenburg and Tannenberg (today Olsztynek, Nidzica and Stębark). The name of the battle, given by the Germans, alluded to the Battle of Grunwald of 1410, known in Germany as the Battle of Tannenberg.
