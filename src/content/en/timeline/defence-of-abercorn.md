On 5 September 1914 a force from [German East Africa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niemiecka_Afryka_Wschodnia) crossed the border of British [Northern Rhodesia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodezja_Północna), today's Zambia. Its objective was Abercorn, today [Mbala](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbala), the northernmost British outpost in this part of Africa. So began the defence of Abercorn, which lasted until 9 September.

## An outpost at the end of the world

Abercorn lay on a plateau, a few dozen kilometres from the southern end of Lake [Tanganyika](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganika_(jezioro)) and right by the border with the German colony. It owed its name to the president of the [British South Africa Company](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brytyjska_Kompania_Południowoafrykańska), which administered Northern Rhodesia. At the start of the war it had only token protection. The local commissioner managed to gather a dozen or so district police, who were joined by about 40 soldiers of the Northern Rhodesia Police under Lieutenant McCarthy.

## The raid

The German force numbered over 300 men: a few officers, several dozen [askaris](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askarysi), and above all irregular ruga-ruga warriors and armed porters. The raiders burnt administrative buildings and the stores of a trading company in the border settlements and repeatedly cut the telegraph line linking Abercorn with Fife, further to the east.

The defenders of Abercorn were many times weaker, but they held their positions. Meanwhile relief was on its way from the interior of the colony: over a hundred soldiers of the Northern Rhodesia Police under Major Henry Stennett. It reached Abercorn on 9 September, the day of another German attack. The reinforced garrison, now about 230 men with three machine guns, drove off the attackers, and the next day the Germans withdrew across the border, pursued by McCarthy's detachment.

## War on the border

The raid was small, but it marked the beginning of a long war on the border between Northern Rhodesia and German East Africa. Over the next two years Abercorn, Fife and the neighbouring posts repeatedly repelled German raids, and the Northern Rhodesia Police grew rapidly: by the beginning of 1915 it already numbered over 800 African soldiers and NCOs. The British were soon also helped by troops from neighbouring [Belgian Congo](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Belgijskie).

Abercorn also earned its place in the history of the war in its last days. On 25 November 1918, two weeks after the armistice in Europe, it was here that General [Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck](/postacie/paul-von-lettow-vorbeck), the undefeated commander of the German forces in East Africa, surrendered.
