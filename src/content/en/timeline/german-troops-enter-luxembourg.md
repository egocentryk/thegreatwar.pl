On Sunday 2 August 1914, shortly after dawn, German troops crossed the border of the [Grand Duchy of Luxembourg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luksemburg). Trains carrying soldiers crossed the bridge over the [Moselle](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozela) at [Wasserbillig](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserbillig), while other units crossed the river at Remich. By about 8.30 a.m. the Germans had already occupied the railway station in the [capital](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luksemburg_(miasto)). The day before they had briefly appeared in Troisvierges, but had been withdrawn.

Luxembourg was a neutral state. Its neutrality had been guaranteed by the powers, Prussia among them, in the [Treaty of London of 1867](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traktat_londyński_(1867)). The small duchy had no army capable of defending it, and its security rested solely on these guarantees. For Germany, however, something else was crucial: important railway lines ran through Luxembourg, essential for moving troops towards France and Belgium.

Grand Duchess [Marie-Adélaïde](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Adelajda) and Prime Minister [Paul Eyschen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Eyschen) protested against the violation of the country's neutrality and sent telegrams of protest to Berlin. In the afternoon they met the commander of the German troops on the Adolphe Bridge in the capital. Faced with the superiority of the German forces, however, they had no means of resistance and had to accept the occupation as a fait accompli.

Chancellor [Theobald von Bethmann Hollweg](/postacie/theobald-von-bethmann-hollweg) justified the occupation of Luxembourg by military necessity, claiming that France had been preparing to enter the duchy. Germany also promised to compensate for all damage. Luxembourg remained under German occupation until the end of the war in 1918, while formally keeping its own government and administration.

The occupation of Luxembourg was the first violation of a small state's neutrality in this war. That same evening Germany delivered an ultimatum to Belgium, and two days later its troops entered Belgian territory.
