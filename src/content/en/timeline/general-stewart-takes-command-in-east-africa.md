On 1 September 1914, with the arrival at [Mombasa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa) of the first troops from India, Brigadier-General James Marshall Stewart took command of the British forces in [British East Africa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brytyjska_Afryka_Wschodnia). He was also the commander of Indian Expeditionary Force "C", sent to defend the colony. The date of 1 September is given by a British chronicle of the events of the war; modern studies say more generally that Stewart and his troops reached Mombasa in September.

Stewart was an experienced Indian Army officer who had previously served, among other places, on India's turbulent North-West Frontier. In East Africa he now had under him both the arriving Indian units and the local forces: companies of the King's African Rifles and volunteer settler formations. His main task was to defend the railway from Mombasa to [Nairobi](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi) against German raids.

By the end of October Stewart had assembled about 4,000 soldiers on the border with [German East Africa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niemiecka_Afryka_Wschodnia). At the beginning of November 1914, at the same time as the landing at Tanga, he attacked the German positions at Longido at the foot of [Kilimanjaro](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilimandżaro), but the attack ended in failure. In 1916 he commanded a division in the offensive of General [Jan Smuts](/postacie/jan-smuts), who blamed him for allowing the Germans to slip out of an encirclement and removed him from command. Stewart ended the war as commander of the British garrison in [Aden](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden).
