On 20 August 1914 German troops entered [Brussels](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruksela). The capital of [Belgium](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgia) was undefended: the government had moved to Antwerp three days earlier, and the Belgian army had withdrawn from the Gete to the fortress. The city had been declared open to spare it destruction and casualties.

## An endless parade

The German entry was a show of strength. Units of the German 1st Army of General [Alexander von Kluck](/postacie/alexander-von-kluck), heading west towards France, marched through Brussels. Columns of infantry, cavalry and artillery filed through the streets for many hours, and according to eyewitnesses the march-past went on for several days. The inhabitants watched it in silence. For many of them it was their first sight of an army whose strength no one in Belgium had been able to stop.

## Burgomaster Max

The Burgomaster of Brussels, Adolphe Max, tried to protect the inhabitants from reprisals. He ordered weapons to be handed in, barricades to be dismantled and the civic guard to be disbanded, so as not to give the Germans a pretext for the kind of violence that had befallen other Belgian towns. At the same time he firmly defended the inhabitants' rights against the occupier. For opposing German demands he was arrested in September 1914 and imprisoned in Germany until the end of the war. He became one of the symbols of Belgian resistance.

## The occupation

The capture of Brussels meant that almost all of Belgium was under occupation. The Germans set up the Government General of Belgium with its seat in the capital. The occupation lasted more than four years and brought the inhabitants requisitions, food shortages, censorship and later also deportations of workers to labour in Germany. Feeding the population was largely ensured by international relief, organised with the participation of the neutral United States.

Brussels was liberated only in November 1918. On 22 November King Albert I made a ceremonial entry into the capital at the head of the Belgian army.
