On 13 November 1914 Lieutenant-General Sir Arthur Barrett, [appointed in September](/general-barrett-dowodca-wyprawy-do-mezopotamii) to command Indian Expeditionary Force "D", arrived on the [Shatt al-Arab](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szatt_al-Arab). Some sources state that his transports reached the spot a day later. Barrett took over command from Brigadier-General Walter Delamain, who a week earlier had [landed at Fao](/ladowanie-w-fao) and then pitched camp at Saniya, north of [Abadan](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abadan) island.

With Barrett came the second part of the 6th (Poona) Division: Brigadier-General Charles Fry's 18th Indian Brigade, made up of the 2nd Battalion of the Norfolk Regiment and three Indian battalions, as well as the 10th Brigade, Royal Field Artillery, with three batteries, the 48th Pioneers and two squadrons of the 33rd Light Cavalry. In all this came to about 7,000 men. Delamain kept command of his 16th Brigade.

Barrett's arrival also changed the aim of the expedition. Delamain's task had been above all to protect the Anglo-Persian Oil Company refinery at Abadan. Now that Great Britain was at war with the [Ottoman Empire](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Osmańskie), the force was to occupy [Basra](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basra), the main port and administrative centre of the Ottoman province on the Persian Gulf.

Barrett's soldiers were still coming ashore when scouts were already reporting that the Turks were gathering at Saihan, a few kilometres from the British camp. Barrett decided not to wait until the whole force had been unloaded from the ships. As early as 15 November Delamain set out to break up this concentration.
