On 2 November 1914 [Serbia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Królestwo_Serbii) declared war on the [Ottoman Empire](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Osmańskie). It did so on the same day on which [Russia declared war on Turkey](/rosja-wypowiada-wojne-turcji), Russia being Serbia's chief protector. The government in Niš, as in August when it [declared war on Germany](/serbia-wypowiada-wojne-niemcom), stood beside its allies against another state that had joined Austria-Hungary.

Serbs and Turks were divided by fresh memories of war. In the [Balkan Wars](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojny_bałkańskie) of 1912–1913 Serbia, together with Bulgaria, Greece and Montenegro, had taken almost all the empire's possessions in Europe, occupying among other things Kosovo and a large part of Macedonia. Serbia signed its peace treaty with Turkey in Constantinople only on 14 March 1914, just a few months before the outbreak of the world war. Peace between the two states thus lasted less than eight months.

The declaration had no military significance. After the Balkan Wars the Ottoman Empire had kept in Europe only a scrap of Thrace around Constantinople, and Bulgaria and Greece separated it from Serbia. The two armies never clashed in this war. Serbia in any case had no strength for new fronts. Since August it had been repelling successive Austro-Hungarian offensives, and its exhausted army, short of ammunition, was just then [withdrawing from the Drina](/serbowie-wycofuja-sie-znad-driny) into the interior of the country.

In Constantinople the war meant the end of the Serbian diplomatic mission. On 11 November the Turkish police entered the Serbian legation, as they did the British and French embassies, searching for hidden radio stations. The ambassador of the neutral United States, [Henry Morgenthau](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morgenthau_Senior), protested, and on 15 November had the legation building sealed.

A state of war with Turkey also arose in the case of [Montenegro](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Królestwo_Czarnogóry), although here the matter is less clear. Some studies state that Montenegro declared war on Turkey on 3 November 1914, while in other lists of the period this declaration does not appear. According to the historian of the Balkan Wars Richard Hall, Montenegro never signed a peace treaty with Turkey after the Balkan Wars. Either way, in the fatwa on holy war proclaimed in Constantinople on 14 November, Serbia and Montenegro were named as enemies of Islam alongside Russia, Great Britain and France.
