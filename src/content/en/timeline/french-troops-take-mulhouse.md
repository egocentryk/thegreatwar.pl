On the evening of 8 August 1914 units of the French VII Corps entered [Mulhouse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miluza), the largest town in southern [Alsace](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzacja). For the first time since 1871, when the province passed under German rule, French soldiers had taken an important Alsatian town.

## Joy in Mulhouse and in Paris

Mulhouse, a centre of the textile industry, was a town with strong ties to France. Some of its inhabitants greeted the soldiers as liberators, showering them with flowers. In France the news caused euphoria. Newspapers wrote of the first great success of the war, and the capture of Mulhouse was to herald the early recovery of the whole of Alsace and Lorraine.

The commander-in-chief, [Joseph Joffre](/postacie/joseph-joffre), issued a proclamation to the inhabitants of the province. In it he addressed the "children of Alsace", writing that after forty-four years of painful waiting French soldiers once again trod their noble soil. He called them the first workers of the great work of revenge.

## Counter-attack

The euphoria lasted less than two days. The commander of VII Corps, General Louis Bonneau, advanced cautiously and feared a German response. Rightly so: reinforcements of the German 7th Army were approaching from [Strasbourg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasburg). On the morning of 9 August the Germans struck in the area of Cernay, west of Mulhouse. The French were in danger of being surrounded.

Bonneau gave the order to retreat, and on 10 August the French troops left Mulhouse, withdrawing towards [Belfort](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfort). A French reserve division sent to help arrived too late. Inhabitants who had openly welcomed the French faced reprisals after the Germans returned.

## Significance

Joffre regarded the retreat as a humiliation and immediately removed Bonneau from command, accusing him of indecision. He was the first of many generals dismissed in the first weeks of the war. The French returned to Mulhouse on 19 August, but this time too they had to leave it.

The brief occupation of Mulhouse became a symbol of the hopes and illusions of the first days of the war. It showed how deeply the French longed to recover the lost provinces, but also how hard that would be to achieve. Mulhouse returned to France only in November 1918.
