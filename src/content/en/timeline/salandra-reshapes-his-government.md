At the end of October 1914 a government crisis broke out in Italy. On 30 October the Treasury Minister Giulio Rubini resigned, and the next day, 31 October, Prime Minister [Antonio Salandra](/postacie/antonio-salandra) handed King [Victor Emmanuel III](/postacie/wiktor-emanuel-iii) the resignation of the whole cabinet. The British chronology of the war records the fall of the government under 30 October. This was not, however, the end of Salandra, but a way of reshaping the government.

## Money for the army

The immediate cause of the crisis was a dispute over military spending. Italy remained [neutral](/wlochy-oglaszaja-neutralnosc), but its army was poorly prepared for war. The Chief of the General Staff, General [Luigi Cadorna](/postacie/luigi-cadorna), demanded that stocks and armament be replenished quickly. Already in October the Minister of War, General Domenico Grandi, had resigned because of disputes with him and been replaced by General Vittorio Zupelli. At the end of the month the government approved an extraordinary credit of 600 million lire to prepare the army and navy. Rubini, a supporter of neutrality, demanded that this expenditure be covered by new taxes. Salandra decided that he needed ministers more committed to war preparations and induced him to leave.

## The new cabinet

The King did not look for a new prime minister. On 2 November he again entrusted Salandra with forming a government, and on 5 November his second cabinet was sworn in. The Foreign Ministry, which Salandra had been running on an interim basis since [the death of Antonino di San Giuliano](/umiera-antonino-di-san-giuliano), was taken over by [Sidney Sonnino](/postacie/sidney-sonnino), twice a former prime minister and one of the most respected liberal politicians. The new Treasury Minister was Paolo Carcano and the Finance Minister Edoardo Daneo. Zupelli kept the Ministry of War.

## Significance

The reshaped government was cohesive and more inclined to prepare for war than its predecessor. Salandra and Sonnino continued the policy of neutrality while negotiating with both sides and trying to obtain as much as possible for Italy. Talks with Vienna on territorial concessions led nowhere, and on 26 April 1915 Sonnino signed the secret [Treaty of London](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traktat_londyński_(1915)) with the Entente powers. In May 1915 Italy entered the war against Austria-Hungary.
