On 31 July 1914 general [mobilisation](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilizacja) began throughout the [Russian Empire](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Rosyjskie). The decision had been taken [the previous day](/car-zarzadza-powszechna-mobilizacje-armii-rosyjskiej) by Tsar [Nicholas II](/postacie/mikolaj-ii), after long hesitation and a conversation with the Foreign Minister, Sergey Sazonov. In the morning, notices appeared in the cities of the empire calling on reservists to report to their units, and the mobilisation telegrams had reached all the military districts.

The mobilisation covered the whole [Russian army](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armia_Imperium_Rosyjskiego), the largest in Europe, including the districts bordering [Germany](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarstwo_Niemieckie). Russia thereby abandoned the idea of a limited mobilisation aimed solely at Austria-Hungary. For Berlin this was a fundamental change: German war plans assumed that Germany had to strike first, before the Russian military machine got fully into its stride.

News of the Russian mobilisation reached Berlin before noon. The reaction was immediate. That same day Germany proclaimed a state of imminent danger of war, and in the evening it sent Russia an ultimatum demanding that all military preparations be halted within twelve hours.

Nicholas II still tried to save the peace in his correspondence with Emperor [Wilhelm II](/postacie/wilhelm-ii). In a telegram of 31 July he explained that halting military preparations was technically impossible, but gave assurances that as long as talks with Austria-Hungary continued, Russian troops would take no offensive action. In Berlin these assurances were no longer taken seriously.

German staff officers expected that Russia would need many weeks to mobilise its forces fully. The mobilisation, however, went more smoothly than expected. By mid-August Russian troops had entered [East Prussia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prusy_Wschodnie), forcing Germany to transfer some of its forces from the Western Front.
