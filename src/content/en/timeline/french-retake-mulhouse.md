On 19 August 1914 French troops entered [Mulhouse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miluza) in [Alsace](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzacja) for the second time. Only nine days earlier they had been forced to abandon the town after a German counter-attack. Now they returned in much greater strength.

## The Army of Alsace

After the first setback the Commander-in-Chief, Joseph Joffre, removed General Bonneau from command and created a special Army of Alsace. Its commander was General [Paul Pau](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pau), an experienced officer who had lost an arm in the Franco-Prussian War of 1870. The Army of Alsace, reinforced with reserve divisions and Alpine chasseurs among others, went on the offensive on 14 August, at the same time as the great offensive in Lorraine.

This time the French advanced more cautiously and in greater strength. On 19 August heavy street fighting broke out in the suburbs of Mulhouse, in the district of Dornach. The French captured the district and entered the town, taking many prisoners and capturing guns.

## A brief triumph

The recapture of Mulhouse was one of the few French successes of August 1914. Once again, however, the joy was short-lived. The very next day the offensive at Morhange and Sarrebourg in Lorraine collapsed, and a few days later the French were defeated in the Ardennes and at Charleroi. The German armies marching through Belgium threatened northern France.

Joffre needed every available force to defend the country. Around 25 August the French left Mulhouse, and the Germans entered the town again. The Army of Alsace was disbanded and its units were transferred to other sectors of the front.

## Significance

The second capture of Mulhouse showed that the French could fight effectively in Alsace when they had adequate forces. The fate of the war, however, was being decided elsewhere, in the north. Until the end of the war France held only a small part of southern Alsace, and Mulhouse remained in German hands until November 1918.
