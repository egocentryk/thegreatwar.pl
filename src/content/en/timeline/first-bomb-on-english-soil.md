On Christmas Eve 1914, at about 10.45 a.m., a German seaplane appeared over Dover once again. This time its bomb did not fall into the sea, as it had [three days earlier](/pierwszy-niemiecki-nalot-na-anglie), but landed on the town. It was the first aerial bomb to fall on land in England. No one was killed and the damage proved slight, but the news quickly went round the British press.

The aircraft was a Friedrichshafen FF 29, number 204, of the naval air unit stationed at Zeebrugge in Belgium. According to some sources it was flown, as on 21 December, by Leutnant von Prondzynski. The bomb was dropped from a height of about 1,500 metres and was most probably aimed at [Dover Castle](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamek_w_Dover), the medieval fortress towering over the harbour, which was still in military use. It missed. It fell about 300 metres from the castle walls, in Thomas Terson's garden in Taswell Street, behind the rectory of St James's Church.

The explosion blew a crater in the ground about three metres long and about one and a half metres deep. The blast broke windows in the surrounding houses, including the rectory. The rectory gardener, James Banks, who was just cutting holly for Christmas, was thrown out of the tree, but suffered no serious injuries. According to some accounts a boy living nearby was also slightly injured, but most sources state that the raid caused no casualties.

The British sent up aircraft in pursuit, but the raider was flying high, in the clouds, and managed to escape out to sea. The next day, Christmas Day, another German seaplane ventured over the Thames estuary and reached the neighbourhood of Erith on the outskirts of London. A British aircraft fired on it in the air, and the German dropped two bombs, which fell harmlessly near the railway station at Cliffe, and turned back.

For Dover this was only the beginning. By the end of the war 184 aerial bombs and 23 shells from German warships had fallen on the town, and 23 people were killed in these attacks. The spot where the first bomb fell is marked today by a plaque.
