On 9 August 1914 in the [North Sea](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morze_Północne), near the island of [Fair Isle](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Isle) between Orkney and [Shetland](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szetlandy), the British light cruiser HMS *Birmingham* rammed and sank the German submarine [U-15](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_U-15_(1911)). It was the first German [submarine](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okręt_podwodny) sunk by an enemy warship.

## Hunting the Grand Fleet

On 6 August ten German submarines sailed from their base at Heligoland. They were to find and attack the ships of the British [Grand Fleet](/sily-zbrojne/grand-fleet) in the North Sea. It was the first organised use of U-boats in the war. Submarines were then a new weapon, untested in combat, and neither side yet knew how greatly they would change the war at sea.

On 8 August U-15 fired a torpedo at the British battleship HMS *Monarch*, but missed. It was one of the first torpedo attacks by a submarine in this war.

## The ramming

On the morning of 9 August the crew of HMS *Birmingham*, part of the 1st Light Cruiser Squadron, spotted in the fog a submarine lying stopped on the surface. U-15 had probably suffered an engine failure, and the British are said to have heard the sounds of repairs coming from inside her. The cruiser opened fire, and when the boat began to dive, *Birmingham* charged at full speed and struck her with her bow, cutting her in two. U-15 sank with all hands.

At the same time another boat from the group, U-13, also went missing. Her fate is unknown; she probably struck a mine or suffered an accident.

## Significance

The sinking of U-15 was a success for the British, but the German submarines' sortie itself caused alarm. It showed that U-boats could operate far from their bases, as far as the waters near Orkney, where the Grand Fleet was based. The base at Scapa Flow did not yet have defences against submarines, and fear of their attack soon led the British to move the fleet temporarily to safer anchorages. A few weeks later German submarines proved their effectiveness by sinking several British cruisers.
