Around 21 August 1914 a detachment of troops from [German South West Africa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niemiecka_Afryka_Południowo-Zachodnia), today's Namibia, crossed the border of the [Union of South Africa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Związek_Południowej_Afryki). The Germans occupied a hill near the post of Nakob, on the desert borderland along the [Orange River](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oranje), and began digging trenches there.

## A disputed date

The incident was minor, but it had great political significance, which is why its details were already being argued over in 1914. The Johannesburg press wrote about German trenches on 21 August. The Minister of Defence, [Jan Smuts](/postacie/jan-smuts), maintained that the Germans had entered the Union's territory as early as 19 August. The leader of the opposition, General [Barry Hertzog](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Hertzog), for his part accused him of exploiting the unclear course of the border to prove German aggression.

## A war not everyone wanted

The Union of South Africa was a British Dominion, created in 1910, a mere eight years after the end of the Boer War. At the beginning of August London asked the government in Pretoria to seize the German ports and wireless stations in South West Africa. Prime Minister [Louis Botha](/postacie/louis-botha) and Smuts agreed. Many Boers, however, were opposed to fighting alongside the British, against whom they themselves had recently fought, and some of them were sympathetic to Germany.

The news of Germans on the South African side of the border helped the government convince public opinion that the Union was defending its own territory, and not merely carrying out London's orders. In September parliament backed the expedition against the German colony.

## What came next

The preparations for the invasion led in October 1914 to a rebellion by some Boer officers and soldiers, which Botha's government suppressed. The campaign in South West Africa ended in July 1915 with the capitulation of the German forces in the colony.
