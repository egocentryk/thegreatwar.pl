On 2 November 1914, according to the British nomenclature of battles, the Battle of [La Bassée](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Bassée) came to an end. The British II Corps, which [three weeks earlier](/poczatek-bitwy-pod-la-bassee) had set out from Béthune to outflank the Germans from the north, had not taken the little town. Exhausted and depleted, it handed over its trenches to soldiers from India. The end of the battle, like that of the [simultaneous fighting at Messines and Armentières](/koniec-bitew-pod-messines-i-armentieres), is a conventional date: fighting continued on this sector, but the weight of the German attacks had shifted north, to Ypres.

## The loss of Neuve-Chapelle

The final weeks of the battle were spent on the defensive. After the withdrawal on the night of 22–23 October, II Corps held a line from Givenchy through [Neuve-Chapelle](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuve-Chapelle) to Fauquissart. The Germans, reinforced by new divisions and heavy artillery, attacked almost daily. On 26 and 27 October they broke into Neuve-Chapelle and after fierce fighting among the ruins took the whole village. On 28 October soldiers of the 47th Sikhs and Indian sappers, among others, counter-attacked. They crossed several hundred metres of flat, open ground and reached the village, but without support they could not hold it. The Sikhs lost more than 200 of their 289 men. Neuve-Chapelle remained in German hands until March 1915.

## The Indian Corps takes over the front

From 29 October the trenches of II Corps were taken over by the two divisions of the Indian Corps: the Lahore Division, whose units [had been fighting in Flanders for several days](/pierwsi-zolnierze-indyjscy-na-froncie), dispersed along the most threatened sectors, and the newly arrived Meerut Division. The relief took two nights, because no communication trenches led to the front line. On 31 October command of the whole sector from Givenchy to Fauquissart was assumed by the commander of the Indian Corps, General James Willcocks.

The soldiers from India immediately had to repel German attacks. On 2 November the Germans struck north-west of Neuve-Chapelle and drove a battalion of Gurkhas out of its trenches, exposing the flank of the neighbouring battalion. The line was restored only by counter-attacks, and over the following days the old trenches were filled in and abandoned. According to English-language studies, by 3 November the Indian Corps had lost nearly 2,000 men.

## The balance

According to the British official history, II Corps lost about 14,000 killed, wounded and missing between 12 and 31 October, the 3rd Division alone more than 5,800. Of the 24,000 infantrymen the corps should have numbered, about 14,000 remained. Even so, there was no question of rest. The remnants of the corps were sent north to help the cavalry and III Corps, and its battalions were soon fighting at Messines and Wytschaete.

The Battle of La Bassée ended without a decision. The British did not outflank the German wing, and the Germans, although they had taken Neuve-Chapelle, did not break through the front. La Bassée remained in German hands until October 1918. The line between Givenchy and Fauquissart on which the Indian Corps stood survived almost unchanged until the spring of 1915.
