On 15 August 1914 the government of [Japan](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarstwo_Wielkiej_Japonii) sent an ultimatum to Germany. It demanded the immediate withdrawal of all German warships from Japanese and Chinese waters and the handing over to Japan of the entire German leased territory of Kiautschou in China, with the port of Tsingtao, today's [Qingdao](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao). Germany was to reply by noon on 23 August.

## Britain's ally

Since 1902 Japan had been bound to Britain by an [alliance](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sojusz_brytyjsko-japoński). After war broke out in Europe, London asked Tokyo for help in combating German warships in the Pacific. The Japanese authorities quickly concluded that the war offered them an exceptional opportunity. Germany was far away and could not defend its possessions in Asia, and taking them over would strengthen Japan's position in China and the Pacific.

## The terms of the ultimatum

Japan demanded that Germany hand over Kiautschou by 15 September, formally with the intention of later returning the territory to China. The form of the ultimatum also carried symbolic weight. In 1895 Germany, together with Russia and France, had forced Japan to give up part of its gains after its victorious war against China. Now Tokyo was dictating terms to Berlin.

## War in the Far East

Germany left the ultimatum unanswered. On 23 August Japan declared war on it. Japanese and British troops besieged Tsingtao from September, and on 7 November 1914 the German garrison capitulated after the [siege](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblężenie_Tsingtau). The Japanese fleet also occupied the German islands in the Pacific north of the equator: the Marianas, the Carolines and the Marshall Islands.

## Significance

Japan's entry into the war sealed the fate of Germany's possessions in Asia and forced Admiral von Spee's squadron to flee across the Pacific. Japan gained far more from the war than many of its European allies. After the war it kept the former German islands, and the question of Kiautschou caused a serious conflict with China. The transfer of the territory to Japan at the peace conference in 1919 provoked mass protests in China.
