On 5 September 1914 the British chronicle of the war recorded that German troops had evacuated [Lille](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille). The great industrial city in northern France, declared an [open city](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miasto_otwarte) earlier, had been undefended since the end of August, and [German cavalry patrols](/niemiecka-kawaleria-zajmuje-lille) had been appearing in the area.

On 2 September the first larger German detachment entered the city. According to local sources, the Germans held members of the municipal authorities as hostages to ensure the obedience of the inhabitants. After about three days they left. Holding the city was not important to them at the time, because the main forces of the German right wing were marching south, towards the Marne and Paris, and every soldier was needed at the front.

The inhabitants' relief did not last long. After the Battle of the Marne the front moved north, and both sides tried to outflank each other in the race to the sea. At the beginning of October the Germans returned to Lille, defended by a small French garrison. After a heavy bombardment the city capitulated and was occupied on 13 October. This time the occupation lasted four years, until October 1918.
