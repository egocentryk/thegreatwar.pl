On the morning of 4 August 1914 two German warships appeared off the coast of French [Algeria](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algieria): the battlecruiser [SMS Goeben](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Goeben) and the light cruiser [SMS Breslau](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Breslau). Goeben shelled the port of Philippeville, today's [Skikda](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukajkida), and Breslau the port of Bône, now [Annaba](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annaba). It was one of the first naval actions of the war.

The ships were commanded by Rear-Admiral [Wilhelm Souchon](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Souchon), commander of the German Mediterranean Division. His aim was to disrupt the transport of French troops from North Africa to France. Transports carrying soldiers of the Army of Africa, needed on the Western Front, were due to sail from the Algerian ports. Souchon learned of the declaration of war on France while still at sea, on 3 August, and decided to strike.

As they approached the coast, the German ships hoisted Russian colours to avoid being recognised too early, and only ran up their own just before opening fire. The bombardment caused damage in the ports and casualties among the population, but it did not stop the French troop transports, which resumed after a short delay.

After the attack Souchon turned east. He coaled at [Messina](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesyna) in Sicily and soon slipped away from the pursuing British Mediterranean Fleet. On 10 August both ships reached the Dardanelles, and soon afterwards they were formally transferred to the Ottoman navy. The escape of Goeben and Breslau had great political consequences: it contributed to the Ottoman Empire's entry into the war on Germany's side.
