On 21 August 1914, on the river [Sambre](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambra) near the Belgian town of [Charleroi](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleroi), a battle began between the French 5th Army of General Charles Lanrezac and the German 2nd Army of General [Karl von Bülow](/postacie/karl-von-bulow). It was one of the most important engagements of the [Battle of the Frontiers](/bitwy/bitwa-graniczna), which decided the course of the first weeks of the war in the west.

## Warnings that went unheeded

From the start of the war Lanrezac had been warning the high command that the Germans were marching through Belgium in much greater strength than had been thought, and that their right wing could outflank the French from the north. For a long time Joffre refused to accept this. Only at the last moment was the 5th Army shifted north, into the angle of the Sambre and the Meuse. To its left the British Expeditionary Force, which was just approaching Mons, was to take up position.

## The fight for the crossings

The French did not manage to occupy the line of the river fully. On 21 August the German advance guard, including units of the Guard, seized several bridges and crossings over the Sambre between Namur and Charleroi, among other places in the area of Auvelais and [Tamines](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamines). French counter-attacks attempting to throw the Germans back across the river ended in heavy losses. At the same time, to the east, German artillery began shelling the fortress of Namur, which covered the junction of the Sambre and the Meuse.

The next day fighting flared up along the whole length of the front. At Tamines, which was fiercely fought over, on 22 August the Germans shot several hundred inhabitants, accusing them of taking part in the fighting.

## What came next

The battle lasted until 23 August. When the German 3rd Army forced the Meuse at Dinant and threatened the French right wing, Lanrezac ordered a retreat. The defeat on the Sambre, together with the setbacks in the Ardennes and at Mons, began the Great Retreat of the Allied armies, which halted only on the Marne.
