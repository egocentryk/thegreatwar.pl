On 12 August 1914 [Britain](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zjednoczone_Królestwo_Wielkiej_Brytanii_i_Irlandii) and [France](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/III_Republika_Francuska) declared war on [Austria-Hungary](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Węgry). Sources differ on the exact date of the French declaration, giving 11 or 12 August, but in both capitals the decisions were taken practically simultaneously. With this the circle of declarations of war between the great powers of Europe was closed.

## A strange delay

Although the whole crisis had begun with Austria-Hungary's conflict with Serbia, for more than a week after the outbreak of war with Germany London and Paris maintained relations with Vienna. The Habsburg monarchy had not directly attacked either France or Britain, and its army was fighting in the Balkans and preparing for a clash with Russia.

The situation changed when on 6 August Austria-Hungary declared war on [Russia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Rosyjskie), the ally of France and Britain. France also accused Vienna of sending its troops to help Germany. On 10 August Paris broke off diplomatic relations with Vienna, and two days later both Entente states announced that they were at war with the Habsburg monarchy. The Austro-Hungarian ambassador in London, Count Albert Mensdorff, who had tried to save the peace to the last, left Britain.

## War on every front

From 12 August all the Entente powers, France, Britain and Russia, were fighting both Germany and Austria-Hungary. For the Habsburg monarchy this meant above all a threat at sea. The French and British fleets in the Mediterranean could now blockade the Austro-Hungarian fleet in the Adriatic. As early as 16 August French warships sank the Austro-Hungarian cruiser [SMS Zenta](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Zenta) off the coast of Montenegro.

## Significance

The declaration of war on Austria-Hungary was above all of political importance. It showed that the war was no longer a series of separate conflicts but a clash of two blocs: the Central Powers and the Entente. On land, French and Austro-Hungarian troops did not meet for a long time. Direct clashes came only later, among others on the Balkan front and, from 1915, on the Italian front, where Italy joined the Entente.
