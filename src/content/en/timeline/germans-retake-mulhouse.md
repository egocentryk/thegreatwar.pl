Contemporary war chronicles record the recapture of [Mulhouse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miluza) by German troops under the date of 11 August 1914. The French, who had entered the town on the evening of 8 August, left it as early as 10 August, and the Germans returned immediately after their departure.

## Counter-attack

After taking Mulhouse, General Louis Bonneau's French VII Corps found itself in a dangerous position. Reinforcements of the German 7th Army were approaching from [Strasbourg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasburg). On 9 August the Germans struck in the area of Cernay, west of the town, threatening the French units with encirclement. Bonneau, fearing he would be cut off from Belfort, gave the order to retreat.

On 10 August the French troops left Mulhouse and withdrew towards [Belfort](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfort). The reserve division that the commander-in-chief Joseph Joffre had sent to help arrived too late. The town, which only two days earlier had greeted the French with flowers, was once again in German hands.

## Disappointment

In France the news was received with bitterness. The joy at the first victory, as the capture of Mulhouse had seemed, quickly turned into disappointment. Joffre regarded the retreat as a sign of indecision and removed Bonneau from command. He formed a new Army of Alsace under General Paul Pau, which was to renew the offensive.

## Further fighting

The French returned to Mulhouse on 19 August, this time in greater strength. The defeats in Lorraine and the Ardennes and the German thrust through Belgium, however, forced them to withdraw a second time as well, at the end of August. Mulhouse remained in German hands until the end of the war in November 1918.
