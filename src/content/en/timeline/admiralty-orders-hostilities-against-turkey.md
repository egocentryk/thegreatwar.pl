On 31 October 1914 at 5.05 p.m. the British Admiralty sent a short order to all ships: "Commence hostilities at once against Turkey. Acknowledge." Great Britain had not yet declared war on the [Ottoman Empire](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Osmańskie), and its ambassador was still in Constantinople. Even so, the [Royal Navy](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy) was given a free hand.

The order was a response to the [Ottoman fleet's attack on Russian ports](/turecka-flota-atakuje-rosje) on the Black Sea and to the [fruitless demands](/ultimatum-aliantow-wobec-turcji) that Turkey send away the Germans from *Goeben* and *Breslau*. The deadline set by London had passed without a reply, and the Entente ambassadors [had already asked for their passports](/zerwanie-stosunkow-z-turcja). The First Lord of the Admiralty, [Winston Churchill](/postacie/winston-churchill), was in favour of a swift blow. On the back of the telegram with the British ultimatum he added in red pencil an instruction to examine the possibility of bombarding the forts of the [Dardanelles](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardanele), because "it is good to strike a quick blow". His advisers were sceptical, believing that a bombardment would achieve little compared with the risk to the ships.

Churchill nevertheless got his way. On 1 November Vice-Admiral Sackville Carden, commanding the squadron at the entrance to the strait, was instructed to carry out a demonstration at long range, without exposing his ships. On the morning of 3 November the British battlecruisers *Indomitable* and *Indefatigable* and the French battleships *Suffren* and *Vérité* shelled the outer forts of the Dardanelles for some ten minutes or more. One shell hit the magazine of the fort by the village of [Sedd el Bahr](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedd_el_Bahr), which blew up. Several dozen Turkish soldiers were killed.

The bombardment was of little military importance, but it warned the Turks and their German advisers that the strait could become a target of attack. They began to strengthen its defences, above all the minefields. Great Britain formally declared war on the Ottoman Empire only on 5 November 1914, together with France.
