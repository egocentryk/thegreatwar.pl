On 23 August 1914 Germany broke off diplomatic relations with [Japan](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarstwo_Wielkiej_Japonii). At noon that day the ultimatum expired in which Tokyo had demanded the withdrawal of German warships from Far Eastern waters and the handing over to Japan of the leased territory of Kiautschou with the port of Tsingtao.

## An ultimatum without a reply

The government in Berlin had no intention of meeting the Japanese demands and did not reply to the ultimatum at all. The German ambassador in Tokyo, Count Arthur von Rex, conveyed through the American embassy that Germany could not accept Japan's terms and that he and his staff would leave the country by the first available ship. Thus ended his mission in Japan, which had lasted since 1911. The protection of German interests in Japan was taken over by the neutral United States.

## What followed

The breaking off of relations meant war. That same day Japan declared war on Germany, and a few weeks later its troops began the siege of Tsingtao, today's [Qingdao](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao).
