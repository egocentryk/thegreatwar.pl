On the morning of 3 November 1914 German battlecruisers appeared off the coast of Norfolk and shelled the area around [Great Yarmouth](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Yarmouth). It was the first sortie by large German warships to the English coast since the beginning of the war. The [raid on Yarmouth](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardowanie_Yarmouth) did little damage, but it showed that the German fleet could approach the British coast with impunity.

## Hipper's sortie

After the defeat in the [Battle of Heligoland Bight](/bitwy/bitwa-w-zatoce-helgolandzkiej) in August, the German command looked for a way to raise morale in the [High Seas Fleet](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochseeflotte). Rear-Admiral [Franz Hipper](/postacie/franz-von-hipper), commander of the battlecruisers, proposed an expedition to the east coast of England. Light cruisers were to lay mines on the shipping lanes off Yarmouth and [Lowestoft](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowestoft), while the heavy ships covered them and shelled the coast.

Hipper's squadron left the Jade estuary on the afternoon of 2 November. It consisted of the [battlecruisers](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krążownik_liniowy) *Seydlitz*, *Moltke* and *Von der Tann*, the armoured cruiser *Blücher* and four light cruisers: *Strassburg*, *Graudenz*, *Kolberg* and *Stralsund*. Other German ships covered the return route. After a night crossing of the [North Sea](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morze_Północne) the Germans reached the British coast at dawn.

## Halcyon and Lively

The waters off Yarmouth were then patrolled by the old torpedo gunboat *Halcyon*, serving as a minesweeper, and the destroyers *Lively* and *Leopard*. At about 7 a.m. *Halcyon* came upon the Germans and challenged them to make the recognition signal. The answer was fire from heavy guns. The gunboat survived the shelling without a direct hit, although splinters destroyed her wireless aerials and wounded three sailors, one of whom died. The destroyer *Lively* covered her with a [smokescreen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zasłona_dymna), one of the first such manoeuvres of the war.

Meanwhile *Stralsund* was laying mines. At about 7.40 a.m. the German ships fired several salvoes towards the land, but the shells fell on the beach at [Gorleston](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorleston-on-Sea) and did no damage in the town. When the mines had been laid, Hipper turned for home.

## Loss of D5

Three British submarines set out from Yarmouth in pursuit: *E10*, *D3* and [*D5*](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_D5). None managed to attack. *D5* struck a mine and sank. Only four or five men were saved, including her commander, Lieutenant-Commander Godfrey Herbert, and about 20 sailors were killed. It is most often assumed that the mine came from the field laid by *Stralsund*, although according to some sources the submarine hit a drifting British mine. Several fishing boats also sank on *Stralsund*'s minefield the same day.

The British reacted late. The Admiralty at first believed that the raid was meant to divert attention from another, more serious attack, and only later sent Vice-Admiral [David Beatty](/postacie/david-beatty)'s battlecruisers to intercept. By then the Germans were far away. In Britain the raid itself was quickly overshadowed, because almost at the same time news arrived of the British squadron's defeat at the [Battle of Coronel](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_pod_Coronelem).

## A Pyrrhic success

In Germany the raid was hailed as a success, and Hipper received the Iron Cross, although he himself thought little had been achieved. In fact the Germans suffered the heaviest loss themselves. Thick fog lay over the Jade estuary and the returning ships had to wait at sea. On the morning of 4 November the armoured cruiser *Yorck*, which had been covering the squadron's return, ran onto a German minefield and sank with several hundred sailors. Yet the ease with which Hipper's squadron had reached the British coast and returned encouraged the German command to undertake another, much bloodier expedition. In December the battlecruisers shelled Scarborough, Hartlepool and Whitby.
