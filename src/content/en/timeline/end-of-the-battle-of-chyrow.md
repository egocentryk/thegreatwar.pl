On 2 November 1914 the Battle of [Chyrów](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chyrów), which had been going on since [13 October](/poczatek-bitwy-pod-chyrowem), came to an end. For three weeks the Austro-Hungarian Second and Third Armies had vainly attacked the positions of General [Aleksei Brusilov](/postacie/aleksiej-brusilow)'s Russian [8th Army](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_Armia_(Imperium_Rosyjskie)) on the hills south of Przemyśl. On the last day of the battle the Second Army was still attacking, and its XII Corps came up close to Stary Sambor. During the night of 2–3 November, however, the Austro-Hungarian troops began to withdraw.

## An order from the Vistula

The end of the battle was decided not by the fighting at Chyrów but by events several hundred kilometres further north. In the second half of October the Germans and Austrians had been defeated [at Warsaw](/koniec-bitwy-pod-warszawa) and [Dęblin](/koniec-bitwy-pod-deblinem), and their armies were falling back westwards. The Russians could now strike from the north at the rear of the forces fighting on the San. As early as 27 October the Austro-Hungarian high command warned the army commanders that the situation on the Vistula was bad. A few days later the order to retreat arrived. For many soldiers who had been fighting for every hill for weeks it came as a complete surprise.

## Retreat and pursuit

Brusilov quickly noticed what was happening. Airmen reported long columns of transport moving west, so he immediately ordered a pursuit. The Russian vanguard caught up with Austro-Hungarian rearguards and took thousands of prisoners. The Austro-Hungarian armies fell back towards the [Dunajec](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunajec) and to the passes of the [Carpathians](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karpaty), and within a few weeks the Russians had reached the approaches to Kraków and the Carpathian passes. The Austro-Hungarian command soon moved the Second Army by rail to the north, to the Częstochowa area, where it was to support the Germans in a new offensive in the Kingdom of Poland.

## Przemyśl alone again

The retreat meant that Przemyśl, [relieved](/odsiecz-przemysla) only a few weeks earlier, would be encircled once more. On 4 November the authorities ordered civilians without food supplies to leave the city, and a few days later the Russians again surrounded the fortress. The Battle of Chyrów, paid for by both sides with enormous losses, gained Austria-Hungary nothing. The gains of the October counter-offensive were lost, and the front returned to the places that the Habsburg armies had reached after their defeat in the [Battle of Galicia](/bitwy/bitwa-galicyjska).
