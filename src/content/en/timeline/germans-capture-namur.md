On 25 August 1914 the Germans took the last forts of the [fortress of Namur](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twierdza_Namur). That day the forts of Emines, Dave and Suarlée surrendered. The Belgian fortress at the confluence of the [Sambre](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambra) and the [Meuse](/miejsca/moza), [attacked on 21 August](/niemcy-atakuja-twierdze-namur), fell faster than anyone had expected.

## Five days of siege

Namur was defended by a ring of nine forts, built at the end of the 19th century to a design by General [Henri Brialmont](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Alexis_Brialmont). The fort garrisons and the Belgian 4th Division, some 37,000 soldiers in all, were under the command of General Michel. Facing them was the group of General [Max von Gallwitz](/postacie/max-von-gallwitz) from the 2nd Army, numbering more than 100,000 men and supported by the heaviest siege artillery: German 420 mm [M-Gerät](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-Gerät) mortars and Austro-Hungarian 305 mm Škoda mortars.

The bombardment, begun on 21 August, smashed the concrete vaults and armoured turrets of the forts, which had been built with much lighter shells in mind. On 23 August the German Guard infantry took the forts of Marchovelette and Cognelée in the north-east and in the evening entered the town. The same day Fort Maizeret fell. General Michel, judging further defence impossible, led the 4th Division out to the south. On 24 August the forts of Andoy, Malonne and Saint-Héribert fell. The last three, Emines, Dave and Suarlée, held out until 25 August.

## The balance

The Belgians lost at Namur, according to various estimates, about 15,000 soldiers: killed, wounded and taken prisoner. More than 5,000 Belgian soldiers were taken prisoner, together with French troops from the few units that had managed to come to the fortress's aid. German losses were many times smaller, amounting to about 900 men. During the days of the town's occupation part of the centre of Namur burned down, including the town hall on the Place d'Armes.

Some of the defenders, however, managed to escape. A column of the 4th Division fought its way through the region between the Sambre and the Meuse to [Mariembourg](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariembourg) and on into France, although its rearguard was surrounded at [Ermeton-sur-Biert](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ermeton-sur-Biert). About 12,000 soldiers reached France and from there returned to Belgium by sea to join the army defending [Antwerp](/miejsca/antwerpia).

## Significance

The fall of Namur opened the way for the Germans along the Meuse into France and deprived the Allies of their last strongpoint in the valley of that river. It coincided with the French defeat in the [Battle of Charleroi](/koniec-bitwy-pod-charleroi) and the British retreat from Mons. It also showed, as Liège had before, that 19th-century fortresses could not withstand modern siege artillery. Soon the heavy mortars were moved to the French fortress of Maubeuge.
