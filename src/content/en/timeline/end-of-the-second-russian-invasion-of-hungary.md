A British chronology of the war gives under 12 December 1914 the end of the second Russian invasion of northern Hungary. Less than four weeks earlier Russian units had [crossed the Carpathian passes again](/druga-rosyjska-inwazja-na-wegry) and descended into the valleys of what is now eastern Slovakia. According to Slovak historians, the last of them withdrew to the northern side of the mountains on 11 December. The next day the Hungarian units that had driven the Russians out of Hungary stood in Nowy Sącz, in the rear of the Russian armies fighting in Galicia.

## Szurmay in the Laborec valley

The invasion was repelled above all thanks to General Sándor Szurmay, a former state secretary in the Hungarian Ministry of Defence. On 25 November, at Nagyberezna (today Velykyi Bereznyi in Ukraine), in the Uzh valley, he took command of a group formed from Honvéd units and reinforcements brought from the interior of Hungary. Hungarian sources link this appointment to pressure from the Prime Minister, István Tisza, who wanted the Russians out of the country at any cost. Szurmay had no front-line experience, and yet within a few days he achieved success. On 27 and 28 November his troops broke through to the Cirocha valley and threatened to encircle the Russians in the town of [Humenné](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humenné). Kornilov's division had to leave the town and fall back towards the passes.

The pursuit continued over the following days. According to Hungarian authors, by 4 December the Russians on this sector had been pushed back behind the crest of the Carpathians, and the [Uzsok Pass](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przełęcz_Użocka) was again in Austro-Hungarian hands. In the Laborec valley and in Zemplén county the fighting ceased.

## Retreat from Sáros

Further west, in Sáros county (Slovak: Šariš), the Russians held out longer. At the turn of November and December they took Zborov and [Bardejov](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardejów), and their units appeared around Svidník and Stropkov. The decision came only when, in the west, near Kraków, the Austrians went over to the counter-offensive. From 7–8 December the Austro-Hungarian Third Army of General [Svetozar Boroević](/postacie/svetozar-boroevic) advanced northwards through the passes of the [Low Beskids](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beskid_Niski), towards Gorlice, Dukla and Nowy Sącz. Szurmay's group, moved to the left wing of this army, set off towards Bardejov. The Russians left the town without a fight around 9 December. Szurmay's superiors in fact criticised him for losing two days on the march to Bardejov instead of striking straight at Nowy Sącz.

Szurmay pushed on northwards, and on 12 December his 38th Honvéd Division, advancing from Nawojowa, linked up in Nowy Sącz with the units fighting in the [Battle of Limanowa](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-limanowa). Threatened with being cut off, the Russian armies began to retreat behind the Dunajec, and the front moved far away from the Hungarian border.

The second expedition across the Carpathians lasted less than a month. Like the first, it brought the Russians no lasting gains, but it left behind devastated and burnt villages, and thousands of inhabitants of the northern counties once again had to flee from the war. The fighting for the Carpathian passes did not end in December, however. As early as February 1915 the Russians again took Medzilaborce and Bardejov.
