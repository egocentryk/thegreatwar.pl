Around 21 August 1914 the battle on Cer mountain and on the river Jadar was decided. The Austro-Hungarian troops that had entered Serbia on 12 August were beaten and began to retreat across the border rivers, the [Drina](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina) and the Sava.

## The invasion collapses

General [Oskar Potiorek](/postacie/oskar-potiorek), commanding the Austro-Hungarian forces in the Balkans, had counted on a swift punitive expedition against Serbia. His armies, however, dispersed their strength, advancing from several directions across difficult, mountainous terrain. The Serbian commander-in-chief, Vojvoda [Radomir Putnik](/postacie/radomir-putnik), concentrated his troops against the most dangerous thrust. In night and day fighting on the ridge of Cer mountain, which had gone on since 15 August, the Serbs smashed the Austro-Hungarian VIII Corps.

From 19 August the morale of the Austro-Hungarian soldiers began to crack. Columns fell back in disorder towards the Drina, abandoning equipment, and many soldiers drowned during the crossing. Potiorek had to order a retreat. The Austrians held out longest at [Šabac](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Šabac) on the Sava, which the Serbs recaptured on 24 August.

## Crimes against civilians

The retreat revealed the scale of the violence committed by Austro-Hungarian troops against Serbian civilians, especially in the [Mačva](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mačva) region. Villagers, including women and children, were shot and hanged on charges of partisan warfare. These crimes were investigated and described, at the invitation of the Serbian government, by the Swiss criminologist Archibald Reiss.

## The first Allied victory

The Battle of Cer was the first Allied victory of the First World War. A small state, weakened by the Balkan Wars, defeated the army of a great power. The commander of the Serbian Second Army, [Stepa Stepanović](/postacie/stepa-stepanovic), was promoted to the rank of vojvoda for it. Austria-Hungary suffered heavy losses and lost prestige, and the war that was to have been a short reckoning with Serbia was only beginning. As early as September Potiorek renewed the attack, which led to the [Battle of the Drina](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_nad_Driną).
