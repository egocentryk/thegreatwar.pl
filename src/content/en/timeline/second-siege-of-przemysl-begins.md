On 10 November 1914, according to the British chronology of the war, the second siege of the [fortress of Przemyśl](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twierdza_Przemyśl) began. Barely a month after its [relief](/odsiecz-przemysla), Russian troops once again surrounded the fortress on the [San](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/San). The encirclement took several days, so dates in the literature differ. Polish studies usually begin the second siege on 5 November, and other sources on 8 or 9 November, when the ring closed completely.

## The army leaves again

At the beginning of November the Central Powers' counter-offensive in Galicia collapsed. After the defeat of the Germans and Austrians on the Vistula, the Austro-Hungarian armies broke off the [Battle of Chyrów](/koniec-bitwy-pod-chyrowem) and the fighting on the San and began to retreat westwards and into the Carpathians. For the second time Przemyśl was left alone. The fortress was to tie down as many Russian forces as possible and bar their way through Galicia, as it had done in the autumn during the [first siege](/bitwy/pierwsze-oblezenie-przemysla).

This time it was less well prepared. In October masses of field troops had passed through Przemyśl and drawn on its depots, and the stocks could not be fully replenished, even though more than two hundred supply trains reached the fortress. On 4 November the authorities ordered civilians who had no food stocks and were not essential to leave the city. The evacuation was carried out in haste and many inhabitants remained in the city.

## Who stayed in the fortress

According to Polish studies, the encirclement trapped some 131,000 soldiers of General [Hermann Kusmanek von Burgneustädten](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Kusmanek_von_Burgneustädten), more than 20,000 horses and about 2,000 Russian prisoners. Estimates of the number of civilians vary widely, from over 18,000 to 30,000. The garrison consisted mostly of older reservists from the many nations of the monarchy, and its strongest unit was the Hungarian 23rd Honvéd Infantry Division.

The fortress was surrounded by the Siege Army of General [Andrei Selivanov](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriej_Sieliwanow), later renamed the 11th Army. It consisted mainly of reserve divisions and according to Polish estimates numbered about 100,000 men. Selivanov did not intend to repeat the bloody assault of October. He decided to cut the fortress off from supplies and starve it into surrender.

## The first sorties

Kusmanek did not wait passively. As early as 14 and 20 November a dozen or so battalions of the garrison sallied from the fortress and attacked the Russian positions to the west of it, towards Bircza. These sorties could not break the blockade, but they forced the Russians to keep considerable forces at Przemyśl. No one in the fortress yet suspected that the siege would last more than four months and end in one of the greatest capitulations of this war.
