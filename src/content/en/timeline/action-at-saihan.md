On 15 November 1914 the 16th Indian Brigade of Brigadier-General Walter Delamain attacked Turkish troops at Saihan, on the west bank of the [Shatt al-Arab](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szatt_al-Arab), a few kilometres from the British camp at Saniyah. It was the first sizeable engagement of Indian Expeditionary Force "D" since the [landing at Fao](/ladowanie-w-fao).

Two days earlier [General Arthur Barrett had arrived](/barrett-obejmuje-dowodztwo-w-mezopotamii) with the second part of the force and orders to take [Basra](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basra). Scouts reported that the Turks were gathering at Saihan. They were estimated at about 1,200 men with machine guns and mountain guns. Barrett ordered Delamain to drive them off, but without getting drawn into a long fight, as his own troops were still disembarking.

Delamain took with him the 2nd Battalion, Dorsetshire Regiment, the 104th Wellesley's Rifles and a battery of mountain guns, while the 20th Punjabis with a second battery formed the reserve. In the morning, as the brigade approached Saihan, the Turks opened fire at a range of more than a kilometre. The fighting took place in difficult conditions, in dust, mud and air shimmering with heat, which made it hard to judge distances. The 104th turned the Turkish left flank, while the Dorsets and the Punjabis attacked frontally. The Turks withdrew, and the British occupied and destroyed their camp. In accordance with his orders, Delamain did not pursue the enemy and returned to camp.

The brigade's losses were 9 killed and 53 wounded. Turkish losses are uncertain. Studies give from about 130 to 250 killed and wounded, and some of them judge that there were far more Turks at Saihan than the scouts had believed.

The action at Saihan should not be confused with the battle at nearby Sahil, fought two days later. After the defeat at Saihan the Turks fell back a few kilometres upriver, to an entrenched line between Sahil and Zain. On 17 November (19 November according to some sources) both of Barrett's brigades, supported by warships, attacked them there. After this defeat the Turks abandoned Basra, and on 21 and 22 November British ships and troops entered it.
