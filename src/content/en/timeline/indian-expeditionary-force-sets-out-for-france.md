On 24 August 1914 the first transports of Indian Expeditionary Force A sailed from the ports of [British India](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_Brytyjskie). These were the first units of the Indian Army sent to Europe in this war. At first they were to go to Egypt, but in the end they were sent to the Western Front, in France.

## From Egypt to France

Immediately after the outbreak of war, London asked the Government of India to send two infantry divisions to Egypt. They were to replace the British garrison there and guard the [Suez Canal](/miejsca/kanal-sueski), the empire's key sea route. Mobilisation began on 8 August. The force comprised the Lahore and Meerut Divisions and two cavalry brigades.

Events in France quickly changed the plans. The British Expeditionary Force was small, and after the first battles it urgently needed reinforcements. It was soon decided that the trained professional soldiers from India would be more useful in Europe. Egypt became merely a stage on their journey.

The first transports of the Lahore Division sailed from [Karachi](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaczi) and [Bombay](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbaj) between 24 and 29 August. The first units reached [Suez](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez) on 8 September. One of the division's brigades, the Sirhind Brigade, remained in Egypt for a time, while the rest sailed on. On 26 September two brigades of the Lahore Division landed at [Marseilles](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsylia), where the inhabitants gave them an enthusiastic welcome. In October they were joined by the Meerut Division. The two formations formed the Indian Corps under the command of General James Willcocks.

## At the front

Before leaving for the front the soldiers received new rifles and equipment, because their weapons and kit were not suited to the conditions of a European war. As early as the second half of October the first Indian battalions went into action in Belgium, during the [First Battle of Ypres](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_bitwa_pod_Ypres). On 31 October Sepoy Khudadad Khan of the 129th Baluchis, badly wounded, worked a machine gun to the very last. He became the first Indian soldier to receive the Victoria Cross.

## Significance

The Indian soldiers arrived in France at the most difficult moment for the British, when their professional army was melting away in heavy fighting. They helped to hold the front in the autumn of 1914 and fought at Neuve Chapelle and Loos in 1915. At the end of 1915 the Indian infantry was moved to the Middle East, mainly to Mesopotamia, while the cavalry remained in France until 1918.

Force A was not the first Indian Army unit sent abroad. As early as 19 August the [first Indian force set out for East Africa](/pierwszy-oddzial-indyjski-wyrusza-do-afryki-wschodniej).
