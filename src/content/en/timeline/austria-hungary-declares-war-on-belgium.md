On 22 August 1914 [Austria-Hungary](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Węgry) declared war on [Belgium](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgia). The two states had no direct disputes with each other, and Belgium had been defending itself against the German invasion for three weeks. Vienna's decision was an expression of solidarity with Germany, the Habsburg monarchy's most important ally.

## The justification

In its note declaring war the Austro-Hungarian government made two charges. First, Belgium had rejected the German proposals and then entered into military cooperation with France and Britain, which were at war with Germany. Second, Austro-Hungarian subjects in Belgium had allegedly been treated in a manner contrary to the most elementary laws of humanity. Vienna stated that in this situation it had to break off diplomatic relations and consider itself in a state of war with Belgium.

The note was signed by the Austro-Hungarian minister in Brussels, Prince Siegfried von Clary-Aldringen. Sources give different dates for the declaration of war: British chronicles give 22 August, but according to Belgian documents the note reached the government in Antwerp only on 28 August, as a telegram sent from The Hague through the Dutch foreign ministry.

## Austrian mortars on the Meuse

The declaration of war was above all a formality. The Austro-Hungarian army was fighting in Serbia and Galicia and did not send its divisions to the Western Front. Austro-Hungarian artillery was, however, in action on Belgian soil. Heavy 305 mm Škoda mortars lent to the Germans had already shelled the forts of Namur and later took part in the siege of Antwerp.

## Significance

The declaration of war on Belgium was a further step in the widening of the conflict that had begun in July with Austria-Hungary's war on Serbia. The camps of the Central Powers and the Entente were by now almost fully formed. A few days later, on 25 August, Japan declared war on Austria-Hungary.
