On 23 August 1914, near the Belgian town of [Mons](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mons), the [British Expeditionary Force](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brytyjski_Korpus_Ekspedycyjny) fought its first battle of the war. It was the first time the British had fought in Western Europe since the Battle of Waterloo in 1815.

## Meeting Kluck's army

The force, commanded by Field Marshal Sir [John French](/postacie/john-french), took up positions on 22 August along the Mons–Condé canal, to the left of the French Fifth Army. The British expected to continue their advance. They did not know that heading straight for them was the German First Army of General [Alexander von Kluck](/postacie/alexander-von-kluck), much more numerous than they were.

## Rapid fire

On the morning of 23 August the Germans attacked the canal crossings. The main weight of the fighting fell on General Horace Smith-Dorrien's II Corps. The heaviest fighting took place in the salient formed by the loop of the canal at [Nimy](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimy), where British infantry defended the bridges.

The British soldiers were professionals, trained in rapid and accurate rifle fire. The Germans, attacking in close formation, suffered heavy losses and, according to accounts, believed they were facing numerous machine guns. For the defence of the bridges at Nimy Lieutenant Maurice Dease and Private Sidney Godley received the first [Victoria Crosses](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzyż_Wiktorii) of the war. Dease died of his wounds that same day.

## Retreat

In the afternoon the Germans crossed the canal in several places, and the British fell back to a second line of defence. In the evening French learned that the French Fifth Army had begun to retreat after its defeat at Charleroi. His troops were threatened with encirclement. The next day the Expeditionary Force began to withdraw southwards. It was the beginning of the Great Retreat, which ended only on the Marne.

The British lost about 1,600 men at Mons. German losses were higher, although their exact number remains disputed. The battle quickly became a legend, and soon the story appeared of the [Angels of Mons](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anioły_z_Mons), who were said to have shielded the retreating soldiers.
