On 22 August 1914 the German government agreed to the Belgian proposal that the colonies lying in the African free trade zone, the so-called conventional basin of the Congo, should remain neutral. The reply was transmitted through Spain, which after the outbreak of war represented Belgium's interests in Berlin. On the same day Germany made a similar declaration to the United States ambassador, asking him to pass it on to the governments of France and Belgium.

## A proposal two weeks old

Belgium put forward the neutralisation initiative on 9 August. It invoked the General Act of the [Berlin Conference](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konferencja_berlińska_(1884–1885)) of 1884–1885, which allowed states with colonies in the free trade zone to agree on their neutrality in time of war. Brussels hoped in this way to protect the [Belgian Congo](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Belgijskie) from fighting for which its colonial forces were not prepared. We write more about this proposal in the article [Belgium proposes the neutralisation of the Congo Basin](/belgia-proponuje-neutralizacje-kotliny-konga).

## Consent that came too late

In mid-August the Germans had good reasons to back neutralisation. Their colonies were cut off from the mother country by the British fleet, and Togoland was just falling to British and French troops. The neutrality of Central Africa would have protected above all German East Africa and Kamerun.

Britain and France, however, had no intention of giving up their advantage. They counted on quickly seizing the German colonies, ports and wireless stations. Besides, fighting in Africa was already under way: in mid-August the Germans [took Taveta](/niemcy-zajmuja-tavete) in British East Africa, and on 22 August a German steamer attacked Belgian posts on Lake Tanganyika. The Allies took the view that Germany itself had violated the neutrality of the zone.

## Significance

The neutralisation proposal collapsed. The war engulfed the whole of Central and East Africa, and the fighting in German East Africa went on until November 1918. The Belgian Congo became an active participant in the conflict. Its troops, the [Force Publique](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Publique), fought in Kamerun and East Africa, and in 1916 took Tabora.
