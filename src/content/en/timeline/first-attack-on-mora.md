On 27 August 1914 a British force from [Nigeria](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria) attacked the German post at [Mora](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mora_(Kamerun)) in the north of [German Kamerun](/miejsca/kamerun). The attack failed. It was the beginning of the fighting for Mora, which went on intermittently for a year and a half.

## A mountain fortress

Mora lies about 160 kilometres south of [Lake Chad](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czad_(jezioro)), close to the border with Nigeria. It was garrisoned by a company of the [Schutztruppe](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutztruppe), the German colonial troops, under Captain Ernst von Raben. It numbered a dozen or so Europeans and more than a hundred African soldiers (askaris). On hearing of the war the Germans occupied the mountain rising above the town, with its steep, rocky slopes, and turned it into a natural fortress.

## A failed assault

A British column of soldiers of the Nigeria Regiment set out from [Maiduguri](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiduguri) under Captain R. W. Fox. On 26 August it took up positions near the German defences, and during the night of 26–27 August its soldiers forced their way up the mountain. The defenders, however, drove them back and, according to some sources, also captured a machine gun and a supply of ammunition. British losses were small, but Mora could not be taken. At the same time other British columns from Nigeria were attacking Kamerun at several points, also without success. We write more about the Allied preparations in the article [General Dobell commands the Cameroon expedition](/general-dobell-dowodca-wyprawy-do-kamerunu).

## The longest defence in Kamerun

French troops from Chad soon joined the siege of Mora. The Allies brought up machine guns and artillery, but successive assaults on the rocky slopes failed. Raben, cut off from the rest of the colony, held out for more than a year. He surrendered to General Frederick Cunliffe only on 18 February 1916, when he learned that the main German forces had left Kamerun. He obtained honourable terms, including payment of wages to his askaris. Mora was the last German post in Kamerun to lay down its arms.
