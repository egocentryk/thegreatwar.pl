At the end of October 1914 British cruisers found the German light cruiser [*Königsberg*](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Königsberg_(1905)), hidden in the backwaters of the delta of the [Rufiji](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufidżi) river on the coast of [German East Africa](/bitwy/kampania-wschodnioafrykanska). A British chronology records the event under 31 October, but most accounts state that the ship was spotted as early as 30 October. From then on *Königsberg* was blockaded and could not return to the ocean.

## A hideout in the jungle

After [sinking the cruiser *Pegasus*](/zatoniecie-hms-pegasus) off Zanzibar on 20 September, Commander Max Looff needed a safe place to overhaul his worn-out engines. He chose the shallow, mangrove-covered Rufiji delta, where the British did not expect a large warship. Parts needing repair were taken overland to the workshops in Dar es Salaam. The cruiser was carefully camouflaged, and soldiers, field guns and observation posts linked by a telegraph line were deployed along the riverbanks. Coal was brought to her by the steamer *Somali*.

## A clue in the papers

In the Indian Ocean the German cruiser was being hunted by the British cruisers *Chatham*, *Dartmouth* and *Weymouth* under Captain Sidney Drury-Lowe. On 19 October *Chatham*'s crew seized the German ship *Präsident*, lying a few kilometres upriver from the port of [Lindi](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindi). Documents found on board showed that the ship had been supplying *Königsberg* with coal in the Rufiji delta.

The British directed their search to that area. On 30 October *Königsberg* and *Somali* were spotted in one of the arms of the delta. Sources differ as to which of the cruisers sighted her first. Some name *Chatham*, others *Dartmouth*. The shallow waters, however, did not allow the large ships to get within effective range.

## A long blockade

The three cruisers blockaded the mouths of the river. At the beginning of November the British shelled *Somali* and destroyed her. On 10 November they scuttled the old collier *Newbridge* in the main channel of the delta to block the Germans' way to the sea, although other arms of the river still remained open. *Königsberg* did not try to break out. A siege of the ship hidden in the jungle began that lasted many months, in which the British even used aircraft. The decision came only in July 1915, with two shallow-draught [monitors](/slownik/monitor), *Severn* and *Mersey*.
