On 28 August 1914, according to a British chronicle of the war, the Battle of the Meuse came to an end. For several days the French 4th Army of General [Fernand de Langle de Cary](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_de_Langle_de_Cary) had held the line of the river between Mézières and Stenay against the German 4th Army of Duke [Albrecht of Württemberg](/postacie/albrecht-wirtemberski), supported by the 3rd Army of General [Max von Hausen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_von_Hausen).

After their defeat in the Ardennes the French fell back behind the [Meuse](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moza), as we describe in the article [Battle of the Meuse begins](/poczatek-bitwy-nad-moza). The Germans crossed the river at several points, among them at Donchery near Sedan, but the French counter-attacked and in places threw them back to the water's edge. The fighting was fierce and costly for both sides.

Despite local successes, the 4th Army could not hold on along the Meuse while the armies on either side of it continued to fall back. On the orders of the commander-in-chief, General [Joseph Joffre](/postacie/joseph-joffre), it resumed its retreat southwards. Some sources, especially French ones, extend the battle to 29 August, because on that day rearguard units, among them the Moroccan Division at Signy-l'Abbaye, were still fighting hard. The Battle of the Meuse did not stop the Germans, but it delayed their advance and allowed the French army to withdraw in good order.
