At dawn on 20 September 1914 the German light cruiser [SMS *Königsberg*](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Königsberg_(1905)) entered the harbour of the town of [Zanzibar](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar_(miasto)) and attacked the British cruiser HMS *Pegasus* lying at anchor. Taken by surprise, the ship was unable to defend herself and sank after a short fight.

## A ship under repair

*Pegasus* was an old, small [protected cruiser](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krążownik_pancernopokładowy), built at the end of the 19th century. From the start of the war she had been patrolling the coast of [German East Africa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niemiecka_Afryka_Wschodnia) with other ships and had taken part in the operations begun by [the August bombardment of Dar es Salaam](/poczatek-dzialan-wojennych-w-afryce-wschodniej). In September her worn-out boilers and engines needed urgent repair. Her captain, Commander John Ingles, therefore put in at Zanzibar, a British protectorate off the coast of Africa, and began repairs. The ship lay in harbour with part of her machinery dismantled, unable to put to sea quickly.

*Königsberg*, commanded by Commander Max Looff, had been cruising the western Indian Ocean since the start of the war. As early as 6 August she had sunk the British merchant ship *City of Winchester*. Looff learned that a British warship was lying at Zanzibar and decided to attack her.

## Attack at dawn

On the morning of 20 September *Königsberg* unexpectedly appeared in the roadstead. The harbour entrance was guarded only by an armed tug, which was unable to stop her or warn the crew of *Pegasus*. The German cruiser opened fire from a range of several kilometres. Her more modern guns outranged the old British guns, and the first salvoes hit their target. *Pegasus* returned fire, but her shells fell short of the enemy.

After only a few minutes, a quarter of an hour at most, the British ship was a burning wreck, unable to fight. Ingles ordered the colours struck and the ship abandoned. *Königsberg* shelled the harbour for a little longer and then sailed away. *Pegasus* sank later that same day. Casualty figures vary by source: between 31 and 38 members of the crew were killed and about 55 wounded. The Germans suffered no losses. The British later salvaged six guns from the wreck and used them in the fighting on land.

## The end of *Königsberg*

The success did not change the German cruiser's position. Her engines also needed repair, and Looff had nowhere to go. He hid the ship in the backwaters of the [Rufiji](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufidżi) delta on the coast of German East Africa. At the end of October the British found her and blockaded her. For many months, however, they could not reach her in the shallow channels. Only in July 1915 did two shallow-draught [monitors](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_(okręt)) make their way deep into the delta and badly damage the cruiser. On 11 July the crew blew up the wreck.

*Königsberg*'s war did not end there. The Germans salvaged the guns from the wreck and carried them inland. The sailors of her crew, together with the guns, joined the forces of Lieutenant Colonel [Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck](/postacie/paul-von-lettow-vorbeck), which for the following years waged the campaign in East Africa.
