On 10 September 1914 the German [light cruiser](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krążownik_lekki) [*Emden*](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Emden_(1908)) stopped the Greek steamer *Pontoporos*, carrying coal under charter to the British, in the [Bay of Bengal](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zatoka_Bengalska). It was *Emden*'s first prize in the [Indian Ocean](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Indyjski) and the beginning of one of the most famous cruiser raids in the history of naval warfare.

## A lone raider

*Emden* belonged to the German East Asia Squadron, based at Tsingtao. She was commanded by Fregattenkapitän [Karl von Müller](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_von_Müller). In the very first days of the war the cruiser captured the Russian steamer *Ryazan* in the Sea of Japan, which the Germans converted into an auxiliary cruiser. *Emden* then joined the main force of Vice-Admiral Maximilian von Spee at the island of Pagan in the Marianas, [who had left Pohnpei at the beginning of August](/eskadra-admirala-von-spee-opuszcza-pohnpei).

Spee decided to take the squadron across the Pacific towards the coast of South America. Müller proposed detaching one cruiser for independent operations in the Indian Ocean, through which ran important shipping routes of the British Empire. Spee agreed and chose *Emden*, the fastest ship in the squadron. On 14 August the cruiser, accompanied by the collier *Markomannia*, left the squadron and made her way into the Indian Ocean through the waters of the Dutch East Indies, including the [Lombok Strait](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cieśnina_Lombok). To avoid recognition, the crew rigged a dummy fourth funnel that made the ship resemble British cruisers.

## *Pontoporos*

On 5 September *Emden* entered the Bay of Bengal. The British believed she was still sailing with Spee across the Pacific and did not expect her in these waters. For several days Müller met no ships at all. Only when he moved to the route between [Colombo](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolombo) and [Calcutta](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata) did the lookouts sight a steamer's lights on the night of 9–10 September.

She was the Greek *Pontoporos*, with a cargo of about 6,000 tons of coal. Greece was neutral, but the ship was sailing under contract to the British, so her cargo was contraband of war and could be seized. Sources differ on whether the coal was bound for Calcutta or Bombay. Müller did not sink the ship. Coal was priceless to a raider, so the Germans kept *Pontoporos* as their own collier, and Müller made an agreement with the Greek captain and promised to pay his crew for further service.

## The terror of the Bay of Bengal

The following days brought *Emden* a whole series of prizes. By 14 September the Germans had sunk or stopped several British ships, including the transports *Indus* and *Lovat*. The crews of the sunk vessels were transferred to one of the captured ships and sent to port. Shipping in the Bay of Bengal came to a standstill for a time, and British, French, Russian and Japanese warships joined the hunt for the raider. On 12 October the British cruiser *Yarmouth* intercepted both of *Emden*'s colliers, *Markomannia* and *Pontoporos*, off the coast of Sumatra.

On 22 September *Emden* shelled the oil tanks at [Madras](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ćennaj), and on 28 October she entered the harbour of Penang by a ruse and sank the Russian cruiser *Zhemchug* and the French destroyer *Mousquet* there. In less than three months she sank or captured more than twenty merchant ships. Her raid ended on 9 November 1914 [off the Cocos Islands](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwa_koło_Wysp_Kokosowych), where the Australian cruiser [*Sydney*](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Sydney_(1912)) caught *Emden* and wrecked her. Müller, who took care to spare the lives of the crews of the ships he stopped, earned a reputation even among his enemies as a gentleman of naval warfare.
