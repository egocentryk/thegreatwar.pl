On 24 August 1914 General Charles Lanrezac's French Fifth Army was retreating from the [Sambre](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambra) southwards, towards the French border. Thus ended the Battle of [Charleroi](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleroi), one of the most important [Battles of the Frontiers](/bitwy/bitwa-graniczna) on the Western Front.

## The retreat of the Fifth Army

Lanrezac issued the order to retreat on the evening of 23 August. For three days his army had been fighting General [Karl von Bülow](/postacie/karl-von-bulow)'s German Second Army, which was attacking across the Sambre, and that day General [Max von Hausen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_von_Hausen)'s German Third Army had forced the Meuse near [Dinant](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinant) and threatened the French right flank. From the north-east, meanwhile, came news of the fall of the Namur forts. Staying put meant the risk of encirclement.

On 24 August the French corps, covered by rearguards, withdrew through the region between the Sambre and the Meuse towards a line from [Maubeuge](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maubeuge) to [Philippeville](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippeville) and on towards France. The Germans, themselves exhausted by three days of fighting, were unable to cut them off. Lanrezac's army suffered heavy losses but escaped destruction.

On the morning of the same day the Commander-in-Chief, [Joseph Joffre](/postacie/joseph-joffre), admitted in a report to the Minister of War that the offensive had failed along the whole front. He laid the blame chiefly on commanders and soldiers who, in his view, had not shown the expected offensive qualities in the field. The French armies were now to go over to the defensive and gain time to regroup.

## When did the battle end?

Most modern historians, both French and German, date the Battle of Charleroi to 21–23 August. They take Lanrezac's evening order of 23 August as its end. The British chronicle of the war on which our calendar is based, however, gives 24 August as the day the battle ended. On that day the French troops finally broke contact with the enemy and left the battlefield. Both dates thus describe the same moment: the transition from battle to retreat.

## What came next

The retreat of the Fifth Army exposed the right flank of the British, who on the same day also [withdrew from Mons](/brytyjczycy-wycofuja-sie-spod-mons). On 25 August the last forts of Namur fell. The great retreat of the Allied armies towards the Marne began. At the end of August Lanrezac's army halted once more to strike at the Germans at Guise.
