On 27 August 1914 the Battle of Mechelen came to an end, the first great sortie of the Belgian army from the fortress of [Antwerp](/miejsca/antwerpia). After three days of fighting south of the town of [Mechelen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechelen) (French: Malines), the Belgian divisions withdrew back under the cover of the forts.

King [Albert I](/postacie/albert-i) had led the army out of Antwerp on 25 August, as we describe in the article [Battle of Mechelen begins](/poczatek-bitwy-pod-mechelen). The attack was meant to tie down the German reserve corps watching the fortress and to relieve the French and British, who were just then retreating after the [Battle of the Frontiers](/bitwy/bitwa-graniczna). The Belgians at first pushed the Germans back, but the latter brought up reinforcements and counter-attacked. In the face of growing resistance and losses, the King ordered a retreat.

The sortie brought no territorial gains, but it showed the Germans that the Belgian army in Antwerp was still a threat to their rear. The fighting also had a tragic side effect. On the evening of 25 August, in the confusion caused by the Belgian attack, panic and shooting broke out among German units in Leuven. The Germans blamed civilians for it, which marked the beginning of the [destruction of the town](/niemcy-niszcza-lowanium).
