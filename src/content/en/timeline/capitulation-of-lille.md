On 12 October 1914, after three days of siege and bombardment, the French garrison of [Lille](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille) surrendered. The largest city in northern France, which at the beginning of September [the Germans had briefly occupied and then left](/niemcy-opuszczaja-lille), this time remained in their hands until October 1918.

## A garrison without a chance

Lille, surrounded by outdated forts, had been declared an open city as early as August and stripped of its defences. When, however, the [Race to the Sea](/bitwy/wyscig-do-morza) shifted the front northwards, the French command decided to hold it until units of General Louis de Maud'huy's Tenth Army, then fighting at Arras, could arrive. The defence was entrusted to Lieutenant-Colonel de Pardieu. According to various sources he had under his command between about 2,400 and 3,000 soldiers, mainly older reservists from Territorial units, only a few guns and meagre stocks of ammunition.

Facing them were much stronger troops of the German Sixth Army, including units of the Saxon XIX Corps and [Landwehr](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landwehra) formations. The first shells fell on the city on the evening of 10 October.

## The bombardment

The heaviest shelling came on the night of 11/12 October. According to French sources, the German artillery fired about 5,000 shells, mainly at the districts near the railway station and south-east of the centre. Fires broke out that there was nothing to put out with. Whole blocks along the rue Faidherbe, rue de Paris, rue de Tournai and rue du Molinel burned down. The number of buildings destroyed is given very variously, from about 900 to more than 2,000 houses, shops and tenements. At least a hundred civilians were killed, according to some sources more than two hundred.

The defenders repelled the German attacks but had no chance of resisting for long. Towards evening on 12 October, with their ammunition exhausted, de Pardieu and the last soldiers, shut up in the citadel, surrendered. The German monograph on the Battle of Ypres states that about 4,500 Frenchmen were taken prisoner, probably including soldiers who did not belong to the garrison but merely happened to be in the city. On 13 October the Germans took full control of Lille and posted the first decrees of the occupation authorities.

## Four years of occupation

Lille became an important centre behind the German front, through which troops fighting in Flanders passed. Its inhabitants, as in other occupied cities of northern France, were subject to curfews, requisitions and levies, and machinery and raw materials were carried off from the factories. Hostages were taken, and in 1916 thousands of inhabitants of Lille and neighbouring towns were deported for forced agricultural labour. The Germans left the city only on 17 October 1918.
