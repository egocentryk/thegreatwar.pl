On 23 August 1914 Brigadier-General Charles Macpherson Dobell was appointed commander of the Allied land forces that were to occupy [German Kamerun](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamerun_Niemiecki). British and French units were to fight under his orders, chiefly African soldiers from the neighbouring colonies.

## Kamerun, an Allied target

Kamerun was one of Germany's largest colonies in Africa. It had an important port at [Duala](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duala_(miasto)) and a radio station that could support German warships in the Atlantic. The colony bordered British Nigeria and French Equatorial Africa, and France wanted to recover the territory it had been forced to cede to Germany in 1911 after the Moroccan crisis. In August 1914, as neighbouring Togoland was just falling, the British and French decided to attack Kamerun as well.

## An experienced officer

Dobell, born in Canada, had previously served in many British colonies, including West Africa, where he had commanded units of African soldiers. At the end of August he sailed from Britain with his staff. The first British attacks from Nigeria, made before his arrival, ended in failure.

## The capture of Duala

Dobell's expedition arrived off the coast of Kamerun in September. After naval operations in the estuary of the river Wouri, Duala capitulated to the Allied forces on 27 September 1914. It was the Allies' biggest prize in the first phase of the campaign. The fighting in the interior, in difficult conditions of jungle and mountains, went on for a long time, however. The last German post in Kamerun surrendered only in February 1916.
