On 20 August 1914 Pope [Pius X](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_X) died in Rome. He was 79 and had led the Catholic Church since 1903. He died only a few weeks after the outbreak of a war that divided Europe's Catholics into warring camps.

## A pope of ordinary people

Pius X, born Giuseppe Sarto into a poor family in northern Italy, was a pastor, not a diplomat. As pope he became known for reforms in the life of the Church, including encouraging frequent communion and lowering the age of children's first communion. He also firmly combated so-called modernism, currents in theology that he regarded as a threat to the faith. He was known for the simplicity of his life. According to tradition, he said of himself that he was born poor, lived poor and wished to die poor.

## War and death

The Pope had long feared a great war in Europe. According to those around him, the outbreak of the conflict in August 1914 shook him deeply, and his health deteriorated sharply. He died after a short illness. Among the faithful the belief quickly spread that he had died of grief over the war.

## The successor

At the [conclave](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konklawe_1914) that opened on 31 August, the cardinals on 3 September elected the Archbishop of Bologna, Giacomo della Chiesa. He took the name [Benedict XV](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedykt_XV). He was an experienced diplomat, which was of enormous importance in wartime.

Throughout the war Benedict XV tried to maintain the neutrality of the Holy See and called for peace. He called the war "the suicide of civilised Europe", organised help for prisoners and the wounded, and in 1917 presented the warring sides with his own peace plan. His appeals, however, went unanswered. Pius X was canonised in 1954.
