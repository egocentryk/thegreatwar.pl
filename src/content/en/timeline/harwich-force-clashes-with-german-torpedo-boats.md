On the night of 22–23 January 1917 a series of night actions took place in the waters between Harwich in England and the coast of Holland. British light cruisers and destroyers from [Harwich](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harwich) tried to intercept the German 6th Torpedo Boat Flotilla, which was being transferred from [Heligoland](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helgoland) to [Zeebrugge](/miejsca/zeebrugge) on the Flanders coast. Despite the overwhelming British superiority, most of the German ships got through to their destination. The flotilla's flagship, *V69*, was badly damaged and its commander killed, but the British lost the destroyer *Simoom* with 47 men.

Since the autumn of 1916 German destroyers sent from the High Seas Fleet had been stationed in the Belgian ports; in October they had [raided the barrage in the Strait of Dover](/pierwszy-niemiecki-rajd-niszczycieli-w-ciesninie-kaletanskiej), and in November they had [ventured off the Suffolk coast](/rajd-niemieckich-niszczycieli-u-wybrzezy-suffolk). On the morning of 22 January the Admiralty learnt that in the afternoon another flotilla would leave the German river mouths for Zeebrugge. It owed this knowledge to the cryptanalysts of [Room 40](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_40), who were reading German wireless signals. It was the 6th Flotilla of Korvettenkapitän Max Schultz, a veteran of the [Battle of Jutland](/bitwy/bitwa-jutlandzka): the leader *V69* and ten large torpedo boats, in practice modern destroyers. At 11.40 a.m. Commodore [Reginald Tyrwhitt](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Tyrwhitt) received orders to intercept them. In the afternoon he put to sea with six light cruisers, two flotilla leaders and sixteen destroyers, some of them borrowed from the Dover forces. He deployed them in several groups to bar both possible routes: along the Dutch coast past the Maas and Schouwen Bank lightships, and across the middle of the bight past the North Hinder lightship. The night was clear and freezing, and the decks were soon covered in ice.

At about 2.45 a.m. British time the German flotilla ran into three of Tyrwhitt's light cruisers, *Centaur*, *Aurora* and *Conquest*. Both sides opened fire without switching on their searchlights, since the ships were betrayed by the glow from their funnels. *V69* was hit by a shell that jammed her rudder. The ship began to circle and was rammed by *G41*, which was itself badly damaged. According to German sources a shell also struck *V69*'s bridge and killed Schultz. The rest of the flotilla broke away from the British under cover of a [smoke screen](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zasłona_dymna), while the torpedo boat *S50* lost touch with the others in the confusion and carried on alone. At about 3.40 a.m. a second group of cruisers, *Penelope*, *Cleopatra* and *Undaunted*, came upon the slowly moving *V69* and riddled her with shells at close range in the light of their searchlights. On *Penelope* they were sure they had sunk her, but the searchlights were switched off too soon. The crippled *V69*, with a funnel shot away and a large hole in her stern, limped into the Dutch port of [IJmuiden](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJmuiden). *G41* reached Zeebrugge along the Dutch coast.

After the first clash the British force scattered. Tyrwhitt's orders did not say what the destroyers were to do if the cruisers encountered the enemy, and some commanders left their assigned patrol lines to chase the Germans on their own account. Meanwhile the main body of the 6th Flotilla slipped past the Schouwen Bank lightship unseen a few minutes before four and got beyond the reach of the British. Only the lone *S50* ran into the British destroyers. She exchanged fire briefly with *Simoom*'s group and then hit *Simoom* with a torpedo. *Simoom*'s magazine exploded. The survivors were taken off by the destroyer *Morris*, and in the morning the wreck, which was still afloat, was sunk by the flotilla leader *Nimrod*. According to British casualty lists 47 members of *Simoom*'s crew were killed, including her commanding officer, Commander Edwin Inman. On the cruiser *Undaunted* an iced-up gun burst, killing two sailors. *S50* turned back along the Dutch coast and returned to Germany.

The British official naval history called the outcome of the night "singularly disappointing". Although the Admiralty knew the enemy's movements and had crushing superiority, most of the German ships reached Flanders, inflicting more losses than they suffered. The causes were found in the chaos of night fighting and the lack of clear orders in case contact was lost. A German communiqué of 23 January announced the destruction of one British destroyer in action and of a second, seen later from an aircraft in a sinking condition. The British were unable to prove that *V69* had entered IJmuiden fleeing from pursuit, so the Dutch government refused to intern the ship. After makeshift repairs she returned to Germany in February. Within a few days the reinforced German forces in Flanders put to sea again.
