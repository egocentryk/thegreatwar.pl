On 13 August 1914 aircraft of the [Royal Flying Corps](/sily-zbrojne/royal-flying-corps), the air arm of the British Army, took off from [Dover](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover) and flew across the English Channel to France. They were the first British military units to cross to the Continent by air. They were to support the [British Expeditionary Force](/sily-zbrojne/brytyjski-korpus-ekspedycyjny), which was arriving in France by ship in those days.

## First to arrive

The first to take off, at 6.25 a.m., was Lieutenant Hubert Harvey-Kelly of No. 2 Squadron. After a flight of less than two hours he landed near [Amiens](/miejsca/amiens), which had been designated as the assembly point for the aircraft. His was the first British aeroplane to arrive in France in wartime. According to accounts, Harvey-Kelly took a short cut and landed ahead of his commanding officer, who had intended to be first.

In the course of the day further aircraft of Nos. 2 and 3 Squadrons and part of No. 4 Squadron reached Amiens. No. 5 Squadron soon followed. There were accidents: some machines broke down, and several airmen were killed in crashes even before reaching the front.

## Modest beginnings

British military aviation was small in 1914. A few dozen aircraft of various types were sent to France, most of them underpowered and unreliable. Nobody yet knew exactly how to use them in war. It was assumed that the airmen's most important task would be reconnaissance: watching enemy movements and passing information to the command.

## Significance

It was in precisely this role that aviation quickly proved its worth. At the end of August British airmen spotted the movements of German columns, which helped the command during the retreat from Mons. In September air reconnaissance revealed the change in the direction of march of the German First Army, which contributed to the victory on the Marne. In the following years the small formation of 1914 grew into a powerful air force, and in 1918, after its merger with the naval air service, the Royal Air Force was created.
