British chronicles of the war recorded under the date of 4 August 1914 the conclusion of a secret alliance between [Germany](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarstwo_Niemieckie) and the [Ottoman Empire](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Osmańskie). In fact the treaty had been signed two days earlier, on 2 August, in [Constantinople](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantynopol). It was signed on the German side by the ambassador, Hans von Wangenheim, and on the Ottoman side by the Grand Vizier, [Said Halim Pasha](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_Halim_Pasza).

The agreement was directed above all against [Russia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Rosyjskie), the empire's age-old rival. Both states undertook to remain neutral in the Austro-Serbian conflict, but if Russia entered the war and Germany had to fulfil its obligations to Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire was to side with Germany. Germany promised to defend Ottoman territory, and the German military mission was to have real influence over the command of the Ottoman army.

The treaty was the work of a narrow group of Young Turk leaders, above all the Minister of War, [Enver Pasha](/postacie/enver-pasza). Most members of the government and the parliament knew nothing of it. Officially the Ottoman Empire declared armed neutrality and began to mobilise, keeping its freedom of action.

Over the following months Constantinople delayed entering the war. The situation was changed by the arrival of the German ships *Goeben* and *Breslau*, which on 10 August entered the Dardanelles and were incorporated into the Ottoman navy. At the end of October 1914 these ships, under the Ottoman flag and the command of a German admiral, attacked Russian ports on the Black Sea. At the beginning of November Russia, Britain and France declared war on the Ottoman Empire.
