Germany entered the war on 1 August 1914, when it [mobilised and declared war on Russia](/niemcy-oglaszaja-mobilizacje-i-wypowiadaja-wojne-rosji), and two days later [on France](/niemcy-wypowiadaja-wojne-francji). From the first days it fought on two fronts, and after its defeat [on the Marne](/bitwy/pierwsza-bitwa-nad-marna) it could no longer decide the war quickly. In the east it was victorious and imposed the [Treaty of Brest-Litovsk](/traktat-brzeski) on Russia, but in the west it bled itself white in battles of attrition, while the naval blockade starved the country. After the failure of its last offensives and the outbreak of revolution, on 11 November 1918 it signed the [Armistice of Compiègne](/rozejm-w-compiegne).

## The road to war

German policy rested on the [alliance with Austria-Hungary](/niemcy-i-austro-wegry-zawieraja-dwuprzymierze), concluded in 1879 against Russia, which [Italy joined](/niemcy-austro-wegry-i-wlochy-zawieraja-trojprzymierze) in 1882. After the departure of [Otto von Bismarck](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck), Berlin in 1890 [did not renew the secret treaty with Russia](/niemcy-nie-odnawiaja-traktatu-reasekuracyjnego), and in 1894 Russia [became France's ally](/wchodzi-w-zycie-sojusz-francusko-rosyjski). From then on the German staff prepared for a war on two fronts. Under the [Schlieffen Plan](/slownik/plan-schlieffena) of 1905 Germany was to defeat France within 6 weeks of mobilisation by outflanking it through neutral Belgium.

Under Emperor [Wilhelm II](/postacie/wilhelm-ii) Germany began to build a battle fleet. In 1898 the emperor signed the [first Navy Law](/niemcy-uchwalaja-pierwsza-ustawe-morska) of Admiral [Alfred von Tirpitz](/postacie/alfred-von-tirpitz), which created the German battle fleet. In the Moroccan crises, from the [emperor's visit to Tangier](/wilhelm-ii-laduje-w-tangerze) in 1905 to the [dispatch of the gunboat *Panther* to Agadir](/niemiecka-kanonierka-panther-wplywa-do-agadiru) in 1911, Berlin tried to force concessions from France. Already at the [Algeciras Conference](/konferencja-w-algeciras-konczy-pierwszy-kryzys-marokanski) in 1906 Germany was backed only by Austria-Hungary.

## Entry into the war

After the [assassination in Sarajevo](/arcyksiaze-franciszek-ferdynand-zamordowany-w-sarajewie), on 5 July 1914 Wilhelm II [promised Austria-Hungary full support](/kajzer-obiecuje-austro-wegrom-pelne-poparcie) against Serbia. On 31 July Germany [demanded that Russia halt its mobilisation](/niemcy-wysylaja-ultimatum-do-rosji) within 12 hours, and the next day it ordered general mobilisation and in the evening declared war on Russia. On 3 August it declared war on France, citing invented French air raids. That morning Belgium [rejected the German ultimatum](/belgia-odrzuca-niemieckie-zadania), and on 4 August Germany [marched into its territory](/niemcy-wypowiadaja-wojne-belgii). Chancellor [Theobald von Bethmann Hollweg](/postacie/theobald-von-bethmann-hollweg) admitted that he was breaking international law. The same day [Britain found itself at war with Germany](/wielka-brytania-wypowiada-wojne-niemcom).

War aims were written down only once the war had begun. In a programme of 9 September 1914, drafted on the chancellor's instructions by his secretary Kurt Riezler, France was to cede some northern territory, Belgium was to become a vassal state, and buffer states were to be carved out of land taken from Russia. The whole was to be held together by an economic union, [Mitteleuropa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitteleuropa), dominated by Germany. The historian Fritz Fischer saw this programme as proof that Germany went to war for territorial gain. Most scholars today regard it rather as a discussion document than as adopted government policy.

## At the fronts

The war plan failed in September 1914. The French and British stopped the Germans on the Marne, the armies of the right wing [began to retreat](/niemcy-wycofuja-sie-znad-marny), and the chief of staff, [Helmuth von Moltke](/postacie/helmuth-von-moltke), [was removed from command](/falkenhayn-zastepuje-moltkego) in favour of [Erich von Falkenhayn](/postacie/erich-von-falkenhayn). From then on a line of trenches ran from the Belgian coast to the Swiss border. In the east [Paul von Hindenburg](/postacie/paul-von-hindenburg), recalled from retirement, with [Erich Ludendorff](/postacie/erich-ludendorff) as his chief of staff, crushed the Russians [at Tannenberg](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-tannenbergiem) and [at the Masurian Lakes](/bitwy/bitwa-nad-jeziorami-mazurskimi). In Asia the Germans lost [Tsingtao](/kapitulacja-tsingtao), while in East Africa [Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck](/postacie/paul-von-lettow-vorbeck) laid down his arms only after the armistice.

In 1915 the troops of [August von Mackensen](/postacie/august-von-mackensen) [broke the Russian front at Gorlice](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-gorlicami), on 5 August the Germans [entered Warsaw](/niemcy-zajmuja-warszawe), and in the autumn, together with Austria-Hungary and Bulgaria, they [occupied Serbia](/bitwy/podboj-serbii). In February 1916 Falkenhayn attacked [at Verdun](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-verdun) to bleed the French army white, but lost a comparable number of men himself. In the summer the British and French attacked [on the Somme](/bitwy/bitwa-nad-somma), where the Germans lost between about 450,000 and 600,000 men. At sea the High Seas Fleet clashed with the Grand Fleet [at Jutland](/bitwy/bitwa-jutlandzka) but did not break British superiority. On 29 August 1916 the emperor dismissed Falkenhayn, and the supreme command passed to [Hindenburg and Ludendorff](/hindenburg-szefem-sztabu-generalnego).

In 1917 the Germans in the west [withdrew to the Hindenburg Line](/bitwy/odwrot-na-linie-hindenburga). The way out of the deadlock was to be [submarine warfare](/tematy/wojna-podwodna-1914-1918): on 31 January 1917 Germany [notified](/niemcy-oglaszaja-nieograniczona-wojne-podwodna) the United States that its submarines would sink every ship around the British Isles without warning. Together with the [Zimmermann Telegram](/telegram-zimmermanna), a secret offer of an alliance to Mexico against the USA, this meant that on 6 April [the United States entered the war](/usa-wypowiadaja-wojne-niemcom).

In the east Berlin allowed Lenin [to travel through Germany](/lenin-wraca-do-piotrogrodu) to Russia. On 15 December 1917 Soviet Russia [signed an armistice at Brest-Litovsk](/rozejm-w-brzesciu), and on 3 March 1918 a peace in which it lost Poland, Lithuania, Courland, Livonia, Estonia, Finland and Ukraine.

## The home front

In August 1914 the Social Democrats in the Reichstag also voted for the war credits. In December the only deputy who [voted against further credits](/liebknecht-glosuje-przeciw-kredytom-wojennym) was [Karl Liebknecht](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Liebknecht). Cut off by the blockade, Germany [introduced bread ration cards](/kartki-na-chleb-w-niemczech) as early as February 1915. In the winter of 1916–1917 the potato harvest failed and city dwellers had to live on swedes, while rations at times fell to 1,000 calories a day. This period went down in history as the [Turnip Winter](/zima-brukwiowa). By various estimates the blockade killed between 0.3 and 0.76 million people.

Hindenburg and Ludendorff demanded a sharp increase in the output of weapons and ammunition, known as the Hindenburg Programme, and the Auxiliary Service Law of December 1916 imposed compulsory labour on all men aged 17 to 60. In July 1917 the army leaders, with the help of deputies, [brought down Bethmann Hollweg](/michaelis-kanclerzem-rzeszy), and the Reichstag [called for a negotiated peace](/rezolucja-pokojowa-reichstagu) without forced territorial acquisitions. War-weariness grew. In August 1917 [sailors mutinied](/bunt-w-niemieckiej-flocie) at Wilhelmshaven, and in January 1918 about a million workers across Germany [went on strike](/strajk-styczniowy-w-niemczech), demanding peace and democracy.

## Occupation and the Polish question

At the end of August 1915 the Germans set up the [General Government of Warsaw](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalne_Gubernatorstwo_Warszawskie) in the part of the Kingdom of Poland they had occupied. Its head, General [Hans von Beseler](/postacie/hans-von-beseler), wanted to build a Polish state with an army at Germany's side. On 5 November 1916 Germany and Austria-Hungary proclaimed a [manifesto announcing the creation of a Kingdom of Poland](/akt-5-listopada), with no king and no borders, while a Polish army was to be formed only after the peace. A year later power in the kingdom was handed to a [Regency Council](/patent-o-radzie-regencyjnej), under the strict control of the occupiers. When the legionaries, at the call of [Józef Piłsudski](/postacie/jozef-pilsudski), refused to swear an oath of "brotherhood in arms" with Germany (the [Oath Crisis](/kryzys-przysiegowy)), the Germans [arrested Piłsudski](/aresztowanie-pilsudskiego) and held him in Magdeburg.

Poles from the Prussian partition served in the German army, in which, according to Andrzej Wrzosek's estimate, more than 110,000 of them were killed. On 25 October 1918 [Wojciech Korfanty](/postacie/wojciech-korfanty) [demanded in the Reichstag](/przemowienie-korfantego-w-reichstagu) that the Polish lands of the Prussian partition be joined to Poland, and on 27 December the [Greater Poland Uprising](/bitwy/powstanie-wielkopolskie) broke out.

## The end of the war and the toll

Peace in the east gave Germany its last chance. On 21 March 1918 Ludendorff launched [Operation Michael](/bitwy/operacja-michael), but despite his gains he did not take Amiens, and the offensives that followed did not decide the war either. In July the last German offensive [on the Marne](/bitwy/druga-bitwa-nad-marna) collapsed, and on 8 August the Allies broke the front [at Amiens](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-amiens). On 14 August a [crown council at Spa](/narada-w-spa) concluded that Germany would not win the war militarily, and on 29 September Hindenburg and Ludendorff [demanded an immediate request for an armistice](/ludendorff-zada-rozejmu). The new chancellor, [Max von Baden](/postacie/max-von-baden), [sent a note to Wilson](/noty-pokojowe-do-wilsona).

On 3 November a revolution began [in Kiel](/bunt-w-kilonii), and on 9 November it [reached Berlin](/rewolucja-w-berlinie). The chancellor announced the emperor's abdication and handed power to [Friedrich Ebert](/postacie/friedrich-ebert), while [Philipp Scheidemann](/postacie/philipp-scheidemann) proclaimed a republic. Wilhelm II [took refuge in the Netherlands](/cesarz-wilhelm-w-holandii). At dawn on 11 November the delegation of [Matthias Erzberger](/postacie/matthias-erzberger) signed the armistice in Foch's railway carriage, and at 11 a.m. the guns fell silent on the Western Front. In the [Treaty of Versailles](/slownik/traktat-wersalski) of 28 June 1919 Germany ceded Alsace and north-eastern Lorraine to France and most of Greater Poland and access to the sea to Poland, lost its colonies, and saw its army reduced to 100,000 men. Reparations were later set at 132 billion gold marks.

During the war Germany mobilised between 11 million (according to the US War Department) and 13.25 million men (according to the German government). Between 1.77 million soldiers (by the American count) and 2.04 million (by the German official history) were killed. Together with civilians, including the victims of the blockade, losses came to between 3.4 and 4.3 per cent of the population. Even so, the [stab-in-the-back myth](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legenda_o_ciosie_w_plecy) grew up in the country: the army, undefeated in the field, had supposedly been betrayed by politicians and revolutionaries. Historians reject it, because it was the supreme command itself that demanded the armistice, and [Germany lost the war militarily](/tematy/dlaczego-niemcy-przegraly-wojne).
