France fought the First World War above all on the Western Front, which for more than four years ran across its own territory. Germany declared war on it on 3 August 1914, and the French army, although it lost the Battles of the Frontiers, stopped the Germans on the Marne. In 1918 the French general [Ferdinand Foch](/postacie/ferdinand-foch), as commander of the Allied armies, led them to victory. It cost France the lives of about 1.4 million soldiers.

## The road to war

After its defeat in the war with Prussia, France lost [Alsace and Lorraine](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzacja-Lotaryngia) in 1871, and recovering them remained one of the aims of its policy. For a long time it was isolated in Europe, until on 4 January 1894 [the alliance with Russia came into force](/wchodzi-w-zycie-sojusz-francusko-rosyjski), although St Petersburg had no intention of supporting French hopes of regaining the lost provinces.

In 1904 the [Entente Cordiale](/wielka-brytania-i-francja-zawieraja-entente-cordiale) ended colonial disputes with Britain. Germany tried to break this understanding in two Moroccan crises: in 1905, when [Wilhelm II landed at Tangier](/wilhelm-ii-laduje-w-tangerze), and in 1911, when [the gunboat *Panther* entered Agadir](/niemiecka-kanonierka-panther-wplywa-do-agadiru). From 1907 the French and British armies worked together on plans to move a British Expeditionary Force to France.

## Entering the war

In July 1914 President [Raymond Poincaré](/postacie/raymond-poincare) and Prime Minister René Viviani were on a visit to Russia and returned to France only on 29 July. On the evening of 31 July the socialist leader [Jean Jaurès](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Jaurès), who had called to the last for the crisis to be contained, was shot in Paris. On the afternoon of 1 August France [ordered general mobilisation](/francja-zarzadza-powszechna-mobilizacje), and on 3 August [Germany declared war on it](/niemcy-wypowiadaja-wojne-francji), citing invented French air raids.

In a message to parliament Poincaré declared that the "sacred union" (union sacrée) of the French would not break in the face of the enemy, and at the end of August socialists entered the government for the first time.

## The Western Front, 1914–1916

The French Plan XVII provided for an offensive in Alsace and Lorraine. On 8 August the French [entered Mulhouse](/francuzi-zajmuja-miluze) but had to withdraw after two days, and in the August [Battle of the Frontiers](/bitwy/bitwa-graniczna) their offensives collapsed while the Germans marched through Belgium. 22 August was the bloodiest day in the history of the French army: some 27,000 soldiers were killed. The commander-in-chief, [Joseph Joffre](/postacie/joseph-joffre), lost the Battles of the Frontiers but soon won on the Marne.

On the night of 2–3 September [the government moved to Bordeaux](/rzad-francuski-przenosi-sie-do-bordeaux). In the [Battle of the Marne](/bitwy/pierwsza-bitwa-nad-marna) the French and British stopped the Germans and on 9 September forced them to retreat. After the [Race to the Sea](/bitwy/wyscig-do-morza), by the end of 1914 an unbroken line of trenches ran from Switzerland to the sea.

In 1915 Joffre tried to break the German line in Artois and Champagne. In spring the French Tenth Army attacked [Vimy Ridge](/bitwy/druga-bitwa-w-artois), and on 25 September the [Second Battle of Champagne](/bitwy/druga-bitwa-w-szampanii) and the [Third Battle of Artois](/bitwy/trzecia-bitwa-w-artois) began simultaneously. The French took the first lines and thousands of prisoners, but there was no breakthrough. For France the deadliest year of the war was 1915, with about 350,000 dead.

On 21 February 1916 the Germans attacked [at Verdun](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-verdun). The defence was organised by General [Philippe Pétain](/postacie/philippe-petain), and the battle lasted until 18 December and after ten months ended with the front back almost on its old lines. The French lost some 360,000–380,000 killed, wounded, missing and prisoners there. Even so, from 1 July 1916 they attacked alongside the British in the [Battle of the Somme](/bitwy/bitwa-nad-somma). In December Joffre was replaced by General [Robert Nivelle](/postacie/robert-nivelle), who had earlier retaken the forts at Verdun.

## Crisis in 1917 and victory in 1918

Nivelle promised a breakthrough in 48 hours. His [offensive](/bitwy/ofensywa-nivellea), launched on 16 April 1917 on the Aisne and in Champagne, ended in enormous losses. On 3 May near Soissons soldiers of the 2nd Colonial Division shouted "Down with the war!" and [mutinies swept the French army](/poczatek-buntow-w-armii-francuskiej). On 15 May the government dismissed Nivelle, and [Pétain became commander-in-chief](/petain-naczelnym-wodzem) and brought the mutinies under control. Behind the lines prices were rising: in the first quarter of 1917 by 25 per cent on average.

On 16 November 1917 the 76-year-old [Georges Clemenceau](/postacie/georges-clemenceau) became [prime minister and minister of war](/rzad-clemenceau). He promised war until victory and had politicians accused of defeatism, Louis Malvy and Joseph Caillaux, brought to trial. In spring 1918 the Germans broke through the British front in [Operation Michael](/bitwy/operacja-michael), in May the French front [on the Aisne](/bitwy/trzecia-bitwa-nad-aisne), and from 23 March they [shelled Paris with a long-range gun](/ostrzal-paryza-z-dziala-dalekonosnego).

On 14 April Foch received the title of [commander-in-chief of the Allied armies](/foch-naczelnym-wodzem). In the [Second Battle of the Marne](/bitwy/druga-bitwa-nad-marna), from 15 July, the last German offensive collapsed, and Foch's counter-offensive opened the [Hundred Days Offensive](/bitwy/ofensywa-stu-dni). On 11 November 1918 the Germans signed the [armistice](/rozejm-w-compiegne) in Foch's railway carriage in the forest of Compiègne.

France also fought beyond its own territory. Its ships and soldiers took part in the [Gallipoli campaign](/bitwy/kampania-gallipolijska), and on 5 October 1915 the French 156th Division [began landing](/ladowanie-ententy-w-salonikach) at Salonika. The Entente forces in Macedonia, commanded by Frenchmen, first [Maurice Sarrail](/postacie/maurice-sarrail) and in 1918 [Louis Franchet d'Espèrey](/postacie/louis-franchet-d-esperey), broke the Bulgarian front in September 1918 and forced Bulgaria into the [Armistice of Salonika](/rozejm-w-salonikach). In Africa the French took the German colonies of Togo and Cameroon. In the talks on the partition of Turkey that ended in the [Sykes–Picot Agreement](/porozumienie-sykes-picot), France laid claim to Syria and Cilicia.

## The home front

The front divided the country into three parts: front-line France, occupied France and the rear. The Germans entered ten departments. On 12 October 1914 [Lille capitulated](/kapitulacja-lille), and for the city four years of occupation began. The population of the occupied departments lived on aid from the American Commission for Relief in Belgium, and at Easter 1916 the Germans deported 15,000 women from the occupied areas.

From May 1915 the war economy was run by the socialist [Albert Thomas](/postacie/albert-thomas), as under-secretary of state for artillery, munitions and military equipment. By the end of the war 1.7 million people worked in the armaments industry, including more than 420,000 women. France, which began the war with an antiquated industry, became the arsenal of the Entente. Some 200,000 workers came from the colonies, and 600,000 colonial soldiers joined the army, of whom about 500,000 fought in Europe.

The Polish Army was also formed in France. On 4 June 1917 Poincaré [signed a decree](/dekret-o-utworzeniu-armii-polskiej-we-francji) creating an autonomous Polish army fighting under the Polish flag, and on 20 September 1917 France became the first to [recognise the Polish National Committee](/francja-uznaje-komitet-narodowy-polski) of [Roman Dmowski](/postacie/roman-dmowski), based in Paris. In July 1918 [General Józef Haller](/postacie/jozef-haller) arrived in France, and on 28 September France and the Committee [recognised the Polish Army as allied](/armia-polska-sprzymierzona) and co-belligerent. In November 1918 it numbered about 17,000 men, and most of its later 70,000 joined only after the armistice.

## The end of the war and the cost

The armistice of 11 November obliged the Germans to withdraw their troops within 15 days from France and Alsace-Lorraine, which France regained. During the war between 7.94 million (according to the International Labour Office) and 8.41 million men (according to the US War Department) were called up into the French army.

According to the government report of 1919 the army lost 1.36 million killed and missing, and 1.40 million including the navy and those who died of wounds after the armistice. The US War Department also counted 4,266,000 wounded. Together with civilian victims the war took 4.3–4.4 per cent of France's population.
