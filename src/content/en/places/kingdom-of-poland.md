The [Kingdom of Poland](/slownik/krolestwo-polskie), known as Congress Poland, was in 1914 part of the [Russian Empire](/kraje/rosja) and the most populous part of the Polish lands, which were divided between three [partitions](/slownik/zabory). Jutting far to the west between Prussia and Galicia, in the first year of the war it became one of the main [battlefields on Polish soil](/tematy/wojna-na-ziemiach-polskich). In the summer of 1915 the Russians withdrew from it, taking people and factories with them, and the country was occupied and divided by Germany and Austria-Hungary. In 1916 the occupiers announced a new Kingdom here, and in the autumn of 1918 its lands became the core of an independent Poland.

## The front in the Kingdom (1914)

On 1 August 1914 the border between the Kingdom and Germany [became a front line](/pierwsze-walki-na-granicy-krolestwa-polskiego). The Russians did not intend to defend the western part of the country and pulled out of the border towns, and the Germans entered Kalisz and Częstochowa. On 14 August Grand Duke Nicholas [promised the Poles](/odezwa-wielkiego-ksiecia-mikolaja-do-polakow) the unification of the Polish lands under the Tsar's sceptre. In August Samsonov's [2nd Army](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-tannenbergiem) set out from the Kingdom against East Prussia, while in the south, in the Lublin region, the Russians clashed with the Austrians [at Kraśnik](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-krasnikiem) and [Komarów](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-komarowem).

In the autumn the front swept twice across the middle of the country. In October the Germans stood a dozen or so kilometres from Warsaw, but in the [Battle of Warsaw and Dęblin](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-warszawa-i-deblinem) they had to fall back. In November they struck again and after the [Battle of Łódź](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-lodzia) on 6 December they took that city, the second largest in the Kingdom. The front settled [on the Bzura and the Rawka](/bitwy/walki-nad-rawka-i-bzura), a few dozen kilometres from Warsaw, and for seven months it hardly moved. It was here, [at Bolimów](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-bolimowem), that the Germans first used chemical weapons on a large scale.

## The Great Retreat (1915)

After the breakthrough [at Gorlice](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-gorlicami) in May 1915 it was the Kingdom's turn. On 13 July the Central Powers [struck from the north and the south](/poczatek-wielkiej-ofensywy-panstw-centralnych-na-wschodzie), from the border of East Prussia and towards [Lublin](/miejsca/lublin), which the Russians lost on 30 July. The Russian command decided to withdraw its armies behind the Vistula. On 5 August [German troops entered](/niemcy-zajmuja-warszawe) Warsaw, abandoned by the Russians during the night. Russian rule, which had lasted since 1815, was over. On 20 August the fortress of [Novogeorgievsk](/bitwy/oblezenie-nowogieorgijewska) surrendered, and by the autumn the Russians had left the whole country.

The retreat was a catastrophe for the population. The Russian army practised scorched earth, carried off offices, the university and factory machinery, and drove out the population or pressed it to flee. In this wave of [bezhenstvo](/slownik/biezenstwo) between 2 and 3.5 million inhabitants of the Kingdom and the western provinces fled or were deported into Russia, among them about a million Poles. Many of them did not survive exile.

## Under two occupations (1915–1918)

The victors divided the country into [two governments-general](/slownik/generalne-gubernatorstwo-warszawskie). The German one, with Warsaw and Łódź, was taken over by General [Hans von Beseler](/postacie/hans-von-beseler). It covered about 62,000 square kilometres with 6 million inhabitants. The Austro-Hungarian one, with its seat in Lublin from 1 October 1915, covered 48,000 square kilometres and about 3.5 million people. The Suwałki region came under the military administration of [Ober Ost](/slownik/ober-ost).

The occupiers treated the country as a source of food, raw materials and labour for a Germany cut off by the blockade. Cattle and grain were requisitioned, workers were sent to Germany, and bread was rationed. American food aid [never materialised](/niemcy-odrzucaja-pomoc-zywnosciowa-dla-polski). At the same time the occupiers courted the Poles: in November 1915 a Polish University of Warsaw and Polytechnic were opened, and in 1916 Warsaw [elected a city council](/wybory-do-rady-miejskiej-warszawy).

## A kingdom without a king (1916–1918)

On 5 November 1916 the [manifesto of the two emperors](/akt-5-listopada) was proclaimed in Warsaw and Lublin, announcing the creation from the Kingdom's lands of an "independent state with a hereditary monarchy". It had, however, no king, government or borders, and power stayed with the governors-general. Polish institutions emerged slowly: the [Provisional Council of State](/slownik/tymczasowa-rada-stanu), and from 27 October 1917 the [Regency Council](/rada-regencyjna-obejmuje-wladze) and a government, which took over the courts and schools but had no army.

In the autumn of 1918 the occupation collapsed. On 11 November the German garrison in Warsaw was being disarmed, and the Regency Council [handed military authority to Józef Piłsudski](/odzyskanie-niepodleglosci-przez-polske). The lands of the former Kingdom became the core of the reborn state.
