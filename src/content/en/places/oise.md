The Oise rises in the Belgian province of Hainaut, south of Chimay, and flows south-west across northern France, past Guise, La Fère, Noyon and Compiègne, to the Seine near Paris. At Compiègne it receives its largest tributary, the [Aisne](/miejsca/aisne). During the war the Oise valley was the shortest road from Belgium to the French capital. In 1914 German armies marched down it, then for two and a half years the front cut across it, and in 1918 the southern flank of the German spring offensives fought along the river.

## Retreat and pursuit (1914)

In the last days of August 1914 the French Fifth Army of General [Charles Lanrezac](/postacie/charles-lanrezac) and the [British Expeditionary Force](/sily-zbrojne/brytyjski-korpus-ekspedycyjny) were falling back across the Oise. On 28 August units of the German Second Army attacked the crossings at Guise, and the next day they got over the river. Lanrezac struck at them in the valley and in [the Battle of Guise](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-guise) halted their advance for a day and a half. Late in the evening of 29 August, however, he was allowed to retreat and destroy the bridges, and by 31 August at the latest the Germans had taken the fortress of La Fère where the Serre joins the Oise. Meanwhile the First Army of [Alexander von Kluck](/postacie/alexander-von-kluck) turned towards [Noyon](/miejsca/noyon) and Compiègne and went on south towards the Marne.

After [the Battle of the Marne](/bitwy/pierwsza-bitwa-nad-marna) the Germans halted on the heights beyond the Aisne, and on 12 September the Allies reached the river on the stretch from Compiègne. From 17 September the French Sixth Army attacked along the Oise towards Noyon, and from 22 September the new Second Army of General [Noël de Castelnau](/postacie/noel-de-castelnau) tried to outflank the Germans north of the river in [the First Battle of Picardy](/bitwy/pierwsza-bitwa-w-pikardii). So began the [Race to the Sea](/bitwy/wyscig-do-morza). Neither side turned the other's flank, and the front set on a line from Roye through Lassigny and Ribécourt on the Oise to Soissons.

## The Noyon salient (1914–1917)

For thirty months the front crossed the river at Ribécourt, downstream from Noyon. The Germans held Noyon and the north-eastern part of the Oise department, and the people of the villages near the front line were evacuated. The salient with Noyon at its tip was the point of the German front nearest to Paris, and [Georges Clemenceau](/postacie/georges-clemenceau) reminded his readers of it with the sentence: "The Germans are at Noyon."

In the spring of 1917 the French were preparing a great attack between the Oise and the Avre, but the Germans forestalled them and [withdrew to the Hindenburg Line](/bitwy/odwrot-na-linie-hindenburga). On 18 March the French entered Noyon, and on this stretch the Oise valley was free for the first time. La Fère, however, stayed on the German side, close to the new front line. At the beginning of 1918, at the request of [Philippe Pétain](/postacie/philippe-petain), the British took over from the French a long sector south of [Saint-Quentin](/miejsca/saint-quentin), as far as Barisis beyond the Oise.

## 1918

On 21 March 1918 three German armies attacked from the Oise to near Arras. In the river valley the fog was so thick that columns of soldiers vanished from sight after ten or twelve metres. The Germans crossed the Oise near La Fère and broke the right wing of the British Fifth Army. [Operation Michael](/bitwy/operacja-michael) pushed the front far to the west, and the Eighteenth Army of General [Oskar von Hutier](/postacie/oskar-von-hutier), advancing between the Oise and the Somme, [took Noyon](/bitwy/bitwa-pod-noyon-1918) on 25 March. The French, however, held the hill of Mont Renaud, one of the main bulwarks of the Oise valley. On 25 March France's leaders conferred with the British minister Lord Milner at Pétain's headquarters in Compiègne, and the next day at Doullens the coordination of the Allied armies was entrusted to [Ferdinand Foch](/postacie/ferdinand-foch).

On 9 June Hutier attacked again, between Montdidier and Noyon. In [the Battle of the Matz](/bitwy/bitwa-nad-matz) his troops reached the Oise on 10 June about 10 kilometres from Compiègne, but a counter-attack by General [Charles Mangin](/postacie/charles-mangin) stopped the advance.

In August the French went over to the offensive. In [the Second Battle of Noyon](/bitwy/druga-bitwa-pod-noyon) the armies of Mangin and Humbert attacked between the Oise and the Aisne and on 29 August entered the ruined Noyon. On 13 October the French First Army crossed the Oise and [took La Fère](/francuzi-odbijaja-laon). A month later, on 11 November, in Foch's railway carriage in the forest of Compiègne, not far from where the Aisne flows into the Oise, [the armistice was signed](/rozejm-w-compiegne).
